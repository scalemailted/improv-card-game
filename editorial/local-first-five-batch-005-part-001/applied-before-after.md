## S71-single-a — retained

Choose one symbol, privilege, or position and treat control of it as proof of legitimate authority.

Mechanism: S71 makes possession of one chosen symbol sufficient proof of legitimate authority.

Confusable concept: Ordinary bossiness seeks compliance without needing control of a particular symbol to justify it.

### before

**A:** I'm wearing the barbecue apron. I decide when the burgers turn.

**B:** It's my apron.

**A:** Then you should respect its office.

**B:** I'll take it back.

**A:** That would be a transfer of power. Finish your petition first.

### after

**A:** I'm wearing the barbecue apron. I decide when the burgers turn.

**B:** It's my apron.

**A:** Then you should respect its office.

**B:** I'll take it back.

**A:** That would be a transfer of power. Finish your petition first.

First / second pass: 90 / 88

- cardFidelity: original 5 — A1 makes wearing the apron confer control over cooking; A3 explicitly gives the apron an office. Result 5 — A1 makes wearing the apron confer burger authority; A3 calls it an office; A5 treats repossession as a transfer of power.
- responseLogic: original 5 — B2 claims ownership, prompting A3's distinction between ownership and office; B4's repossession becomes A5's transfer of power. Result 4 — B2's ownership claim becomes A3's obligation to respect the apron; B4's threatened repossession becomes A5's transfer, though the petition is newly invented.
- relationship: original 4 — B2 owns the apron and B4 can reclaim it, giving B practical leverage over A's authority. Result 4 — B2 asserts ownership and B4 threatens to reclaim the apron, giving B a practical means to challenge A's barbecue authority.
- heightening: original 4 — A5 answers B4's threatened removal by requiring a petition, extending apron authority into its own succession. Result 4 — B4 threatens A's actual possession, so A5 escalates from controlling burgers to regulating the transfer of authority.
- specificity: original 4 — A1 establishes a barbecue and a playable dispute over when to turn burgers. Result 5 — A1's barbecue apron and burger timing immediately establish a playable task and the exact object carrying the dispute.
- brevityWit: original 4 — A3's 'respect its office' economically converts B2's property claim into institutional legitimacy. Result 4 — A3's 'respect its office' efficiently reverses B2's ownership claim; A5's petition extends the same apron-based authority.

B4 causes A5: B4 threatens to take the apron back; A5 names that act a transfer of power and imposes a procedural obstacle.

Alternatives: Unlike the meeting-bell alternative's permission trap, this uses an owner who can physically reclaim the symbol and an incumbent inventing transfer procedure.

Reservation: The petition has no prior establishment, and 'finish' presumes one already underway. This is a minor procedural embellishment; no rewrite is needed.
