## S139-single-a — revised

Treat concealment as a precise ritual with passwords, signals, approved phrases, and severe consequences for careless improvisation.

Mechanism: A makes concealment an exact ritual and treats departures from approved communication as grounds for disproportionate consequences.

Confusable concept: Generic secret-agent play adds passwords as decoration; this card makes compliance determine concrete access and penalties.

### before

**A:** Password before we discuss the sandwiches.

**B:** We're in an empty kitchen.

**A:** Then nobody can interrupt the password.

**B:** Is it still cucumber?

**A:** Not aloud. You've triggered the vegetable-reset protocol.

### after

**A:** Write the password before I hand over the surprise picnic sandwiches.

**B:** We're alone in the kitchen, and the bus leaves in two minutes.

**A:** Then write quickly. Speaking it cancels collection.

**B:** I've written cucumber. That's yesterday's password, isn't it?

**A:** You've said it. The sandwiches stay here. I'll tell the walkers their lunch failed clearance.

First / second pass: 62 / 93

- cardFidelity: original 3 — A1 requires a password and A5 invokes a reset, but severe consequences remain unspecified and speaking the requested password becomes arbitrarily wrong. Result 5 — A1 requires a written password, A3 specifies cancellation for speaking, and A5 enforces that consequence.
- responseLogic: original 3 — A3 uses B2's empty kitchen, but A5 penalizes B4 for saying the password A1 requested without establishing a silent method. Result 5 — A3 answers B2's two-minute deadline with faster compliance; A5 applies the spoken-password rule to B4's 'cucumber.'
- relationship: original 3 — B2 questions the need and B4 tries to comply, giving A resistance followed by cooperation. Result 4 — B2 urgently needs lunch released before the bus; B4 attempts compliance while checking the outdated password.
- heightening: original 3 — A5 escalates to a reset protocol, although the practical consequence is only a label. Result 5 — A5 lets the entire group's lunch fail clearance rather than relax the ritual after B4's accidental breach.
- specificity: original 4 — A1's sandwiches and B2's empty kitchen create a clear mundane setting for excessive secrecy. Result 4 — A1 supplies picnic sandwiches and B2 a departing bus; the precise surprise being protected is lightly defined.
- brevityWit: original 3 — A3's uninterrupted password follows B2, while A5's vegetable protocol partly relies on decorative terminology. Result 4 — A5's 'lunch failed clearance' follows the established cancellation rule and the walkers' practical need.

B4 causes A5: B4 says the password aloud, triggering the cancellation rule A3 has already stated.

Alternatives: This enforces a stated breach penalty under transport pressure; alternative b uses gestures and retraining to control access to candle duty.

Reservation: The protected surprise is less concrete than the protocol, and cancellation is a strong beat, though it leaves bargaining available. No rewrite needed.

## S139-single-b — revised

Treat concealment as a precise ritual with passwords, signals, approved phrases, and severe consequences for careless improvisation.

Mechanism: A enforces concealment through precisely specified signals, controlled phrases, and consequential restrictions for careless communication.

Confusable concept: Ordinary surprise-party discretion avoids spoiling the surprise; A builds a ritual qualification system that obstructs preparing it.

### before

**A:** Signal the cupboard with three fingers. Don't say where the cake is.

**B:** The cake's on the table.

**A:** Then use the table signal.

**B:** Which is?

**A:** That information requires the cake briefing.

### after

**A:** Signal Mum's birthday cake location: three fingers for cupboard, four for fridge. Say it aloud and you're off the surprise team.

**B:** It's on the table. The helpers need to find it.

**A:** You're off. Table is a flat palm. I'll signal them.

**B:** I've already sent them a photo. That doesn't say anything.

**A:** It shows everything. Now we move the cake and change the signals. You won't be told where your slice is.

First / second pass: 63 / 90

- cardFidelity: original 3 — A1 specifies fingers and A3 requires a table signal, but A5 supplies no severe consequence for careless disclosure. Result 5 — A1 specifies location signals and expulsion for speaking; A3 enforces that rule; A5 treats photographic disclosure as a breach requiring relocation, revised signals and exclusion.
- responseLogic: original 4 — A3 updates the required signal using B2's table correction; A5 answers B4's request with a briefing prerequisite. Result 5 — B2 names the table and the helpers' need, prompting A3's penalty and flat-palm solution. B4 claims a silent photo is compliant, prompting A5's distinction between silence and concealment.
- relationship: original 2 — B2 corrects a location and B4 asks for information, but B has no reason to move the cake or finish the ritual. Result 4 — B2 wants helpers to find the cake; B4 defends a practical workaround. A3 and A5 constrain that helpfulness, although B mainly serves as the practical foil.
- heightening: original 3 — A5 adds a briefing requirement, increasing bureaucracy without a concrete cost. Result 4 — B4 expands the disclosure to all helpers, so A5 escalates from one person's expulsion to moving the cake, replacing signals and concealing B's slice.
- specificity: original 3 — A1 and B2 establish cupboard, cake, and table, but do not explain the occasion or need for concealment. Result 4 — A1 establishes Mum's surprise birthday cake and playable hand signals. B2 supplies the table location, though A1 delivers a conspicuously dense block of protocol.
- brevityWit: original 4 — A5's cake briefing economically extends A3's signal system and responds to B4's question. Result 4 — A3's 'You're off' promptly pays off A1's rule; A5's withheld slice makes the punishment personal while staying within the concealment logic.

B4 causes A5: B4 reveals an already distributed photo and argues that it says nothing. A5 answers that it shows everything, making relocation and replacement signals necessary within A's protocol.

Alternatives: The preserved picnic alternative uses collection clearance under departure pressure and ends with withheld sandwiches. This alternative uses an active surprise-team relationship, a photographic loophole and a security reset, providing meaningful tactical variation despite their shared food setting.

Reservation: A1 is somewhat expositional, and A5's operational reset rests on A's excessive security logic despite the photo going only to helpers. That excess remains legible as the stance. No rewrite needed.
