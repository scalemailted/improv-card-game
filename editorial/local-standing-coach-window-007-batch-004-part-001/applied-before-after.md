## D121-single-a — revised

Get someone to help contain a secret without first revealing its full risk or purpose.

Mechanism: A recruits practical help concealing a secret while postponing disclosure of what helping entails.

Confusable concept: Delegating evidence handling could look similar, but D121 specifically depends on withholding risk or purpose.

### before

**A:** Hide this envelope under the picnic hamper before anyone comes in.

**B:** What's in it?

**A:** Something easier to explain once it isn't visible.

**B:** Am I involved now?

**A:** Only at the hamper level. We can discuss promotion later.

### after

**A:** Hide this envelope under the picnic hamper before Mum comes in.

**B:** She asked me to unpack the hamper.

**A:** Then keep the envelope in your pocket while you unpack.

**B:** I'll end up taking it home.

**A:** Perfect. I'll explain when she stops asking where it is.

First / second pass: 80 / 90

- cardFidelity: original 5 — A1 recruits concealment; A3 withholds the envelope's purpose and risk. Result 5 — A1 recruits B to hide an envelope from Mum; A5 explicitly postpones explaining why until Mum stops asking.
- responseLogic: original 4 — A3 evades B2's contents question; A5 answers B4's involvement concern. Result 5 — B2 must unpack the hamper, so A3 relocates the envelope to B's pocket; B4 anticipates taking it home, which A5 welcomes.
- relationship: original 3 — B4 worries about involvement, but A5's reassurance changes no practical terms. Result 4 — B2 has a competing task from Mum and B4 identifies an unwanted responsibility; both alter A's concealment arrangements.
- heightening: original 3 — A5 introduces 'promotion' without increasing B4's concrete responsibility. Result 4 — A5 turns B4's accidental removal into an extended concealment plan, keeping B uninformed until the questioning subsides.
- specificity: original 4 — A1 supplies an envelope, hamper and imminent arrivals, though no relationship. Result 4 — A1 establishes the envelope, picnic hamper and Mum's imminent arrival; A3 supplies an immediately playable adjustment.
- brevityWit: original 4 — A5's 'hamper level' neatly minimizes complicity; 'promotion' is more ornamental. Result 4 — A5's 'Perfect' economically converts B4's concern into a benefit for A, with the delayed explanation completing the card logic.

B4 causes A5: B4 predicts taking the envelope home; A5 adopts that outcome and defers disclosure until Mum stops asking.

Alternatives: Unlike single-b's negotiated collection deadline and coded message, this alternative opportunistically turns B's practical difficulties into increasingly useful concealment.

Reservation: B4 offers little resistance, and the envelope's significance stays entirely unspecified. Those limits do not obscure the requested concealment behavior. No rewrite needed.

## D121-single-b — revised

Get someone to help contain a secret without first revealing its full risk or purpose.

Mechanism: A secures another person's silence and custody while offering less information than an informed helper would want.

Confusable concept: A cover story seeks endorsement of an explanation; this card seeks assistance before an explanation is supplied.

### before

**A:** Keep this cupboard key until tomorrow and don't mention the cupboard.

**B:** Why?

**A:** Because a cupboard with a secret key attracts questions.

**B:** You've made me curious.

**A:** Good. Keep the curiosity in your pocket with the key.

### after

**A:** Keep the spare cupboard key till tomorrow. Don't tell your flatmate I've left it here.

**B:** Only if you tell me what's in the cupboard.

**A:** Could you accept knowing what isn't? Nothing alive.

**B:** Then you collect it before she gets home.

**A:** Agreed. Text me when she leaves work. Just 'key', please.

First / second pass: 66 / 92

- cardFidelity: original 5 — A1 asks B to hold the key and remain silent; A3 never reveals the purpose. Result 5 — A1 asks B to conceal custody of the key; A3 offers 'Nothing alive' instead of the requested contents, preserving undisclosed risk.
- responseLogic: original 3 — A3 gives a circular answer to B2; A5 echoes B4's curiosity without managing it. Result 5 — A3 bargains directly with B2's demand for information; A5 accepts B4's collection deadline and requests a departure alert.
- relationship: original 2 — B2 asks 'Why?' and B4 reports curiosity but sets no meaningful condition. Result 4 — B2 demands disclosure and B4 limits participation to collection before the flatmate returns; A must bargain with both conditions.
- heightening: original 2 — A5's pocket metaphor merely repeats A1's request for storage and silence. Result 4 — B4's deadline prompts A5 to recruit B into monitoring the flatmate's movements and sending a deliberately minimal message.
- specificity: original 3 — A1 identifies a cupboard key and tomorrow, but leaves the situation unlocated. Result 5 — A1 establishes a spare cupboard key, a flatmate and tomorrow's deadline; B4 and A5 make the timing and practical task concrete.
- brevityWit: original 3 — A5's curiosity-in-pocket image is compact but substitutes wordplay for a new tactic. Result 4 — A3's 'Nothing alive' is an evasive reassurance, and A5's 'Just key' extends that withholding economically.

B4 causes A5: B4 requires collection before the flatmate arrives; A5 requests notice when she leaves work and restricts the message.

Alternatives: Single-a exploits an accidental opportunity to move evidence; this alternative negotiates informed resistance through partial reassurance and a timed handoff.

Reservation: B4 abandons the original disclosure condition fairly readily, but replaces it with a credible limit. No rewrite needed.

**B:** I took a detour.

**A:** Then give the detour a destination before yours begins.

- cardFidelity: original 4 — A1 and A5 delay departure for an explanation, but the receipt's contradiction is unclear. Result 5 — A1 identifies the six-versus-five contradiction; A3 removes B's transport excuse, and A5 keeps B present to explain the newly admitted stop.
- responseLogic: original 4 — A3 rejects B2's postponement; A5 asks for the destination of B4's detour. Result 4 — B2's last bus prompts A3's offer to drive. B4's admission of a stop prompts A5's question about where, though the original timing contradiction is partly resolved.
- relationship: original 3 — B2 wants to defer the discussion, but B4 offers little beyond 'a detour'. Result 4 — B2 wants to leave and postpone disclosure; A3 accommodates transport while resisting postponement. B4 offers a partial admission that changes A5's demand.
- heightening: original 3 — A5 narrows the question but does not materially develop the ten-minute pressure in A3. Result 4 — After B4 admits stopping elsewhere, A5 makes the ride conditional on disclosure and commits the whole evening to obtaining it.
- specificity: original 2 — A1's 'missing hour on this receipt' does not establish what account is suspicious. Result 4 — A1 supplies a taxi receipt, two conflicting times and an imminent bus departure. A5's invitation to sit is playable, although the location and relationship remain implicit.
- brevityWit: original 3 — A3 and A5 favor balanced phrases about hours and destinations over plain pursuit. Result 4 — A3's concise 'I'll drive you' turns help into a means of prolonging questioning; A5's 'I've got all evening' extends that sincere logic.

B4 causes A5: B4 admits leaving at five and stopping somewhere. A5 specifically demands the stop's location and conditions departure on explaining it.

Alternatives: The preserved wedding alternative uses shared commitments and consequences for invited guests. This candidate uses a helpful transport offer to sustain a private inquiry, giving it a distinct tactic and pressure.

Reservation: A5 largely repeats A3's retention tactic. The admission in B4 supplies a sufficiently specific new omission, but the scene's comic development is modest. No rewrite needed.
