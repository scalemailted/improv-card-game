## S130-single-a — revised

Treat each clue and inconsistency as a contest over who can understand the situation first and most completely.

Mechanism: A treats understanding each clue first and extending that understanding furthest as a competition.

Confusable concept: Credit seeking wants recognition for a contribution; this card makes continuing interpretation itself the race.

### before

**A:** I've found the missing parcel's tracking error. Put my name beside it.

**B:** We were both looking.

**A:** I circled it while you were saying looking.

**B:** Let's fix it together.

**A:** After recording who made together useful.

### after

**A:** I've solved your missing parcel. Tracking says reception; you were still refreshing.

**B:** Reception signed for three boxes. Only two reached our office.

**A:** Then one stayed downstairs. I've narrowed your whole building to one desk.

**B:** The trolley's here. There's a box wedged underneath.

**A:** So it left reception but never got unloaded. You've found the parcel; I've solved why we've been calling reception all morning.

Result 4 — A1 contrasts A's claimed solution with B 'still refreshing'; A3 claims superior narrowing; A5 counters B's discovery by claiming the more complete explanation. Competitive understanding is clear, though A3's certainty outruns the evidence.
- responseLogic: original 4 — A3 counters B2's shared search; A5 makes B4's cooperative fix conditional on attribution. Result 5 — B2's three-box receipt and two-box arrival cause A3's downstairs hypothesis. B4's box under the trolley overturns that hypothesis, and A5 incorporates it into an unloading explanation while reclaiming credit.
- relationship: original 4 — B2 claims joint effort and B4 offers practical cooperation that A obstructs. Result 4 — B2 contributes delivery facts and B4 actually finds the box, pursuing parcel recovery rather than merely feeding boasts. A5 acknowledges B's achievement while competing over what counts as solving.
- heightening: original 2 — A5 repeats the credit demand without introducing another clue or a more complete explanation. Result 4 — After B4 wins the physical search, A5 raises the contest from locating the parcel to explaining the morning's wasted calls. This develops the established competition, though chiefly through a verbal claim.
- specificity: original 4 — A1 establishes a missing parcel's tracking error and an immediate attribution dispute. Result 5 — A1 immediately establishes parcel tracking and reception; B2 supplies the three-versus-two discrepancy; B4 reveals the box wedged under the trolley. Each detail gives actors a clear, playable discovery.
- brevityWit: original 3 — A3's circled-versus-looking line fits rivalry; A5's together-useful phrasing becomes abstract. Result 4 — A3's 'one desk' makes premature certainty compactly comic. A5's distinction between finding and solving follows B4, although the final explanation is longer and more explicit than the earlier boasts.

B4 causes A5: B4 locates the box under the trolley, forcing A5 to abandon the downstairs claim and compete instead over explaining why it remained missing.

## S130-single-b — revised

Treat each clue and inconsistency as a contest over who can understand the situation first and most completely.

Mechanism: A turns every clue and correction into a contest to reach the earliest and most complete explanation.

Confusable concept: Professional suspicion delays action to investigate; competitive solving uses investigation to outrank another person's understanding.

### before

**A:** The mystery guest is the baker. I solved it.

**B:** I told you about the flour.

**A:** A clue is not a conclusion.

**B:** I said it was the baker.

**A:** Yes, but I believed it decisively.

### after

**A:** Our mystery judge is the baker. Flour on the invitation. Solved before you.

**B:** The miller uses flour too.

**A:** Then it's the miller. I've already moved past the obvious answer.

**B:** There's icing on the envelope flap.

**A:** The baker, licking it after decorating. I've got the identity and the sequence; you've got ingredients.

First / second pass: 73 / 86

- cardFidelity: original 3 — A1 declares a solved mystery, but A5 replaces being first or most complete with a claim to decisive belief. Result 5 — A1 claims 'Solved before you'; A3 claims to be ahead despite changing answers; A5 contests completeness of understanding.
- responseLogic: original 5 — A3 distinguishes B2's clue from a conclusion; B4 removes that defense and A5 invents a new one. Result 4 — B2's miller possibility causes A3's switch; B4's icing causes A5 to return to the baker with an added sequence.
- relationship: original 4 — B2 claims the clue and B4 credibly corrects A's account of who named the baker. Result 4 — B2 challenges an inference and B4 supplies discriminating evidence, repeatedly threatening A's claim to superiority.
- heightening: original 3 — A5 intensifies the self-serving claim but stops advancing understanding of the mystery. Result 4 — A5 responds to B4's better clue by claiming both identity and chronology, then reducing B's contribution to ingredients.
- specificity: original 3 — A1 supplies a mystery guest and B2 flour, without establishing the occasion. Result 4 — A1 establishes a mystery judge and flour on an invitation; B4 locates icing on the envelope flap.
- brevityWit: original 4 — A5's decisive belief is a compact response to B4, though it rewards credit grabbing over deduction. Result 4 — A5's 'identity and the sequence' versus 'ingredients' turns B's useful clue into A's competitive accounting.

B4 causes A5: B4 adds icing on the flap; A5 restores the baker and invents a more complete account to reclaim superiority.

Alternatives: Alternative b has an active evidentiary rival and explicit credit competition; a relies on cooperative confirmation and unexplained knowledge.

Reservation: The licking-and-decorating sequence is speculative, but reads as A's competitive overclaim rather than an established deduction. No rewrite needed.
