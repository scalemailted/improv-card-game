# Imprompt Pack Brief — Rules, Rituals & Institutions

**Pack ID:** `rules-rituals-institutions`  
**Reserved IDs:** S169–S192 and D169–D192  
**Target release:** Imprompt v0.15.0  
**In-app status:** Playtest  
**Candidate pool:** 64 cards drafted; 48 selected; 8 held; 8 rejected

## Creative promise

**Rules, Rituals & Institutions turns systems into playable human behavior.** The pack explores what people do when a procedure, custom, standard, record, tradition, or organization shapes what can happen next. Its comedy comes from the friction between human needs and systems that promise order: people enforce rules, seek exceptions, preserve rituals, borrow institutional language, hide inside procedure, and discover that a tiny formality may carry enormous emotional weight.

A card belongs when it gives the holder a clear relationship to a system while remaining portable across settings. The “institution” may be a workplace, family, club, guild, neighborhood, school, team, ceremony, imaginary government, or two people who have accidentally invented a process between them.

## Central comic question

> **What happens when the rule, ritual, or system becomes more important than the immediate human problem?**

The card gives one performer a private lens or engine. It never guarantees that anyone else respects the same rule, understands the same custom, or recognizes the same authority.

## What makes this pack distinct

- **Core Foundations** provides broad scene fundamentals.
- **Everyday Friction** asks why a small inconvenience has become personal.
- **Power Games** asks who gets to decide and what makes authority real.
- **Relationship Knots** asks what remains unresolved between people.
- **Emotional Pressure** asks which feeling reorganizes behavior.
- **Secrets & Schemes** asks how information is hidden, pursued, and traded.
- **Absurd Commitment** asks how an impossible premise becomes coherent and consequential.
- **Rules, Rituals & Institutions** asks how people use, preserve, interpret, resist, or hide inside systems.

A Power Games card is primarily about rank or leverage. A Rules, Rituals & Institutions card is primarily about process, legitimacy, continuity, precedent, or institutional behavior—even when that process also creates power.

## Included territory

- formal rules and unwritten customs;
- procedure, sequence, documentation, records, and approval;
- standards, certification, expertise, and correct terminology;
- institutional memory, precedent, tradition, and continuity;
- bureaucracy, delay, scope, jurisdiction, and responsibility;
- rituals whose form carries emotional or social meaning;
- gatekeeping, exceptions, loopholes, and special treatment;
- reform, interpretation, enforcement, and compliance;
- people using procedure to care, control, compete, conceal, or belong;
- grounded reactions to strange institutions and elaborate customs.

## Excluded territory

This pack should not rely on:

- stereotypes about government workers, teachers, lawyers, religions, or specific cultures;
- mockery of a real faith, ethnicity, disability, or protected identity;
- fixed legal, political, military, or workplace premises;
- paperwork as a static solo activity with no interpersonal engine;
- instructions for real-world fraud, forgery, evasion, or coercion;
- declaring that another performer understands, obeys, or belongs to a system;
- guaranteeing compliance or punishment;
- pure authority struggles better suited to Power Games;
- unrelated weirdness better suited to Absurd Commitment;
- bargains and victory conditions better suited to Competition & Consequences.

## Portability rule

The prompt defines a relationship to process, not a specific institution.

Too narrow:

> You are a city clerk who refuses to approve a building permit.

Portable:

> **The Necessary Signature** — Remain helpful while controlling the one approval, record, key, or ceremonial step that allows anything to move forward.

The portable version can function in a family tradition, spaceship, fantasy guild, wedding rehearsal, neighborhood association, school club, royal court, or completely invented system.

## Hidden-information rule

A Stance may define what the holder treats as official, legitimate, traditional, correct, or required. It cannot declare that another performer knows the rules, has broken a law, belongs to an institution, or will comply.

A Drive may seek approval, information, participation, surrender, or an exception. Resistance must create more scene rather than invalidate the card.

Weak:

> The other performer is under investigation and must confess.

Strong:

> **For the Record** — Get someone to state exactly what they did, knew, or authorized before you reveal your own involvement.

## Desired player experience

A player should quickly understand:

1. what rule, custom, process, or institutional idea matters to the holder;
2. the first observable behavior the card creates;
3. how to respond when a partner ignores or challenges the system;
4. what formality, interpretation, or objective can repeat;
5. how the system can tighten, bend, expand, or reveal human stakes;
6. how to play bureaucracy and ritual without becoming static or merely explanatory.

## Exercise value

Rules, Rituals & Institutions materially expands:

- **Status Clash**, through procedural authority, legitimacy, expertise, gatekeeping, and hidden approvals;
- **Relationship Pressure**, through shared customs, old exceptions, familiar roles, and unresolved rule-breaking;
- **Absurd Commitment**, through ceremonial logic, practical process, and consequences of missing a step;
- **Pursuer & Avoider**, through formal requests, scope deflection, records, approval, and exception seeking;
- **Instigator & Anchor**, through ritual escalation paired with practical deadlines, responsibilities, and compliance;
- custom exercises centered on bureaucracy, tradition, reform, institutional memory, process, or rule interpretation.

## Live-playtest questions

1. Do players make procedure visible through behavior instead of explaining a fictional rulebook?
2. Can every card work outside an office, government, school, or legal setting?
3. Do customs and rituals create relationship stakes rather than only comic formality?
4. Can partners productively ignore, resist, reinterpret, or misunderstand the holder’s system?
5. Are exception-seeking cards distinct from general deception or power plays?
6. Do beginner cards reveal a repeatable behavior within thirty seconds?
7. Do grounded cards remain funny without exaggerated bureaucratic voices?
8. Are any cards too close to Sacred Procedure, The Gatekeeper, The Initiated One, or The Ceremony Requires More?
9. Does the pack deepen Status Clash and Absurd Commitment without becoming redundant with either?
10. Which prompts inspire active scenes, and which risk becoming process monologues?
