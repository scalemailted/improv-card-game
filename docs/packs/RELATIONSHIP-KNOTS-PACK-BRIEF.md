# Imprompt Pack Brief — Relationship Knots

**Pack ID:** `relationship-knots`  
**Reserved IDs:** S73–S96 and D73–D96  
**Target release:** Imprompt v0.11.0  
**In-app status:** Playtest  
**Candidate pool:** 64 cards drafted; 48 selected; 8 held; 8 rejected

## Creative promise

**Relationship Knots turns affection, rivalry, obligation, dependence, and unfinished connection into visible choices that can repeat and heighten.** The pack explores how people preserve closeness, compete for importance, invoke shared history, seek reassurance, protect fragile bonds, reopen old wounds, and negotiate what a relationship now means.

A card belongs when the holder can reveal connection through behavior: using private language, assuming emotional seniority, guarding a ritual, testing memory, requesting proof of loyalty, comparing affection, hiding the cause of distance, turning resentment into warmth, or insisting that a bond must still matter.

## Central comic question

> **What does this connection still require from me—and what do I believe it entitles me to ask from someone else?**

The relationship may be warm, strained, familial, professional, friendly, romantic, rivalrous, ceremonial, or impossible to label. The cards do not define that relationship in advance. They give one performer a private way of understanding and acting within it.

## What makes this pack distinct

Core Foundations supplies broad relationship and objective engines. Everyday Friction turns ordinary responsibilities into pressure. Power Games examines who gets to decide. Relationship Knots focuses on the **emotional structure of connection itself**:

- shared history becomes private language, memory, obligation, or proof of belonging;
- affection can coexist with envy, resentment, control, rivalry, or fear of distance;
- familiar roles return even when both people have changed;
- repair efforts may be sincere, strategic, premature, or self-protective;
- an apology may need to acknowledge emotional cost rather than the event alone;
- loyalty may be tested through memory, presence, sacrifice, priority, or risk;
- boundaries may be interpreted as healthy limits, rejection, or unfinished negotiation;
- small gestures become evidence that a relationship is alive, damaged, unequal, or destined to continue.

## Included territory

- affection, attachment, intimacy, and the need for reassurance;
- rivalry for attention, importance, memory, approval, or special status;
- shared origins, private language, old chemistry, and familiar roles;
- favors, sacrifice, loyalty, promises, and obligations that still feel active;
- dependence that is hidden, minimized, resented, or treated as proof of closeness;
- estrangement, drifting, absence, betrayal, resentment, and partial forgiveness;
- repair, reconciliation, peacemaking, and attempts to define what the relationship is now;
- boundaries, distance, and the fear that connection is becoming less secure;
- keepsakes, rituals, phrases, memories, and habits that symbolize the bond;
- emotional contradictions such as warmth with resentment or care with control;
- connection interpreted as destiny, pattern, burden, privilege, or unfinished business.

## Excluded territory

This pack should not rely on:

- a mandatory romantic, familial, marital, or friendship label;
- a fixed claim that another performer loves, misses, envies, fears, or rejects the holder;
- coerced physical affection or contact;
- trauma disclosure, abuse stereotypes, or diagnostic labels as shortcuts for character;
- a prewritten breakup, reunion, wedding, family dinner, or therapy-session premise;
- generic sadness or longing without observable behavior;
- simple status contests better suited to Power Games;
- pure winning structures better suited to Competition & Consequences;
- secrets or conspiracies whose main engine is concealment rather than relationship;
- instructions that require the partner to forgive, reciprocate, confess, or stay.

## Portability rule

The cards describe a relationship dynamic, not a specific relationship label. Every selected prompt should work between siblings, friends, rivals, coworkers, ex-partners, mentors, neighbors, adventurers, performers, ghosts, or two strangers who quickly establish history.

Too narrow:

> Convince your ex-spouse to take you back at dinner.

Portable:

> **Stay Until We’re Okay** — Keep someone present until the relationship feels repaired, even if the practical reason for staying disappears.

## Hidden-information rule

Each card governs only its holder. A Stance may define how the holder interprets closeness, history, memory, affection, or distance. It cannot declare the other performer’s private feelings or guarantee reciprocity.

Weak:

> Your partner is jealous and wants to be your favorite.

Strong:

> **The Other Favorite** — Treat every sign of attention, trust, and affection as something to compare with what another connection appears to receive.

A Drive may seek reassurance, recognition, apology, loyalty, or repair, but it must remain playable when the partner refuses, misunderstands, or offers a different definition of the relationship.

## Desired player experience

A player should quickly understand:

1. the first visible relationship behavior;
2. what sign of closeness, distance, obligation, or recognition matters to the character;
3. how the behavior can repeat without forcing exposition;
4. what would threaten or complicate the holder’s private understanding of the bond;
5. how the card remains active when the scene partner defines the relationship differently.

The strongest scenes should produce discovery. The performers may begin with conflicting private assumptions—one preserving a ritual, one avoiding the real reason for distance—and discover what the relationship has become through action.

## Metadata additions

This pack adds reusable coach roles:

`attachment-seeker`, `boundary-keeper`, `confidant`, `loyalist`, `memory-keeper`, `peacemaker`, and `reconciler`.

It also adds controlled motifs:

`abandonment`, `affection`, `attachment`, `boundaries`, `closeness`, `connection`, `distance`, `forgiveness`, `intimacy`, `jealousy`, `memory`, `promise`, `reconciliation`, `resentment`, and `sacrifice`.

These remain internal metadata. The visible seven-category system does not change.

## Exercise value

The pack materially deepens existing coach presets:

- **Relationship Pressure** gains six new History & Relationship Stances and six new Secrets & Avoidance Drives;
- **Emotional Stakes** gains six new Emotional Assumptions Stances with attachment, belonging, jealousy, and repair pressure;
- **Crown & Heart** gains richer approval, care, control, and emotional-dependence collisions;
- **Past & Future** gains shared-language, old-role, unresolved-hurt, and relationship-definition material;
- custom exercises can use new roles such as Attachment Seeker, Confidant, Loyalist, Boundary Keeper, Peacemaker, Memory Keeper, and Reconciler.

Every phone still shuffles independently. A shared exercise configuration never reveals cards, deck order, or Scene Log history.

## Playtest questions

1. Does the card create visible relationship behavior rather than a private backstory alone?
2. Can the player understand the instruction in five seconds?
3. Does the prompt work without specifying romance, family, friendship, or another fixed label?
4. Does the card remain playable when the partner offers no matching affection or history?
5. Can the behavior repeat or heighten at least three times?
6. Does the card create connection and responsiveness rather than isolated emotional acting?
7. Does it feel distinct from Core Foundations cards in the same subtheme?
8. Do Mirror exercises create productive emotional collision rather than mutual neediness or stalemate?
9. Do Paired exercises support complementary discovery rather than predetermine reconciliation?
10. Are any cards frequently vetoed because they feel too intimate, vague, coercive, or relationship-specific?
