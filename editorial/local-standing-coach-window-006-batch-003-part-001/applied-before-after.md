## D61-single-a — revised

Hide that you set the problem in motion while quietly directing how everyone responds.

Mechanism: D61 combines hiding A's initiating mistake with quietly retaining control over the response.

Confusable concept: Ordinary blame avoidance can conceal fault without directing anyone's recovery work.

### before

**A:** Let's fix the delivery mix-up. Send the crates back through my office.

**B:** Your email sent them to the wrong city.

**A:** Which means I know the route intimately.

**B:** Should we show the manager the email?

**A:** Show them my corrected routing chart first.

### after

**A:** The crates went to Leeds. Send the driver to me; I'll sort it quietly.

**B:** Your email says Leeds. Shall I forward it to the manager?

**A:** Forward my corrected address instead. She needs the destination, not the correspondence.

**B:** She wants a timeline before approving another van.

**A:** Start with when we discovered the error. I'll arrange the van while you write.

First / second pass: 68 / 88

- cardFidelity: original 3 — A3 acknowledges route knowledge after B2 identifies A's culpability; only A5 attempts concealment by putting the corrected chart first. Result 5 — A3 substitutes the corrected address for A's incriminating email; A5 edits the timeline while directing the recovery.
- responseLogic: original 4 — A3 uses B2's wrong-city accusation, and A5 redirects B4's proposed disclosure toward a different document. Result 4 — B2's email prompts A3's replacement document; B4's timeline requirement prompts A5's selective starting point. Van approval remains slightly unclear.
- relationship: original 3 — B2 identifies responsibility and B4 proposes manager scrutiny, but B has little concrete recovery responsibility. Result 4 — B2 proposes managerial disclosure and B4 introduces an approval constraint, forcing A to manage a colleague with access to evidence.
- heightening: original 3 — A5 manages the order of disclosure after B4, but does not clearly increase A's covert control of recovery. Result 4 — A3 suppresses one email; after B4, A5 attempts to shape the official account of the whole error.
- specificity: original 4 — A1 supplies crates and an office; B2 adds a wrong-city email and A5 supplies a routing chart. Result 5 — A1 immediately establishes misdirected crates, Leeds and a driver; B4 adds a concrete replacement-van approval problem.
- brevityWit: original 4 — A3's 'know the route intimately' economically converts an error into expertise, although concealment remains thin. Result 4 — A3's 'destination, not the correspondence' neatly disguises concealment as operational efficiency; A5 sustains that logic.

B4 causes A5: B4 requires a timeline for approval; A5 chooses a starting point that excludes A's initiating mistake.

Alternatives: This alternative manipulates documents and managerial reporting; D61-single-b accepts extra manual work to prevent automated disclosure.

Reservation: Retain the original. A5's arranging the van needs to mean preparation pending approval, rather than bypassing B4's constraint.
