## D147-single-b — Apologize to Reality

Card: Get someone to apologize to an object, rule, place, or idea for violating its impossible expectations.

**A:** Welcome to our inn. Please apologise to the doorway for knocking after you arrived.

**B:** I had to get here to knock.

**A:** It expected advance notice from your knuckles. Explain that you're sorry.

**B:** Sorry, doorway. I did phone ahead.

**A:** Then apologise for telling the telephone first. It had to hear about its own visitor secondhand.

B4 → A5: B4 introduces the phone call as advance notice. A5 makes that attempted solution a new violation: the telephone learned of the visit before the doorway.

## D150-single-b — What Rule Did I Miss?

Card: Find the hidden rule that makes the strange situation consistent, practical, or at least survivable.

**A:** Your shadow moved before you. What changed?

**B:** I was thinking of leaving, but I can't find the exit.

**A:** Think about that bench without moving. Does it turn?

**B:** It's gone to the bench. I'm still here.

**A:** It seems to follow your intentions. Think about the exit again; let's follow slowly and see whether it knows the way.

B4 → A5: B4 demonstrates that the shadow follows a thought about a specific destination. A5 turns that result into a practical test for finding the exit, while keeping the conclusion tentative.

## D119-single-b — Ready on My Terms

Card: Agree to be honest, hopeful, or vulnerable only in forms that preserve your emotional safety.

**A:** I'll sit by the door while we talk about trying again.

**B:** Are you planning to run?

**A:** Knowing I can leave is helping me stay.

**B:** We can stop when you need to.

**A:** I want to come home. Please don't hand me a key yet; saying it is enough for tonight.

B4 → A5: B4 offers permission to stop. A5 uses that safety to risk the homecoming admission, then preserves a boundary by postponing the key.
