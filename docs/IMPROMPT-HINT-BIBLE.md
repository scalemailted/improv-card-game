# Imprompt Example Bible — v0.25.0

## A spoken scene, not a lecture

A holds the requested card or both requested cards. B is one illustrative partner. B's lines do not prescribe what another real player should say.

Every current example is **A–B–A–B–A**, with no separate stage-action field. Concrete objects, relationships and actions may be established naturally in dialogue. The card stays portable; its illustration can be specific.

The first line gives a playable offer and enough context to understand it. B responds to that offer. A's next line reveals the exact card's influence. B adds a consequence, correction, obstacle, or acceptance. A heightens the same pattern. An offer from B is not just a cue to switch to a stock punchline.

## Editorial criteria

**Specificity:** Could a performer identify the exact card, rather than only its category?

**Continuity:** Does each line answer or develop the previous line? Do roles, objects and facts remain coherent?

**Wit:** Does humour arise from a character's committed logic, recognition, inversion or escalating tactic? Random absurd details do not count as a substitute.

**Effectiveness:** Do A3 and A5 make the stance or pursuit observable? A Drive does not need to succeed. A Stance can be mistaken.

**Brevity:** Prefer 30–65 words across all five turns. This release caps singles at 75 and pairs at 125 in the builder; the actual maxima are lower. Do not pad a thin example simply to reach five lines.

**Listening:** B must contribute something A uses. A5 should respond to B4 rather than repeat the card description.

**Portability:** A concrete scene is one example, not a mandatory premise. Do not interpret specificity as a new restriction on the card.

## Exact pair fusion

For a Stance plus Drive, A holds both. The Stance should change **how A pursues the Drive**. A joke about one card followed by a request from the other is not an adequate bespoke fusion.

The 57,600 exact pairs retain two stored alternatives each. Twenty-five pairs / fifty scenes have internally rewritten bespoke dialogue. The rest remain compiled drafts. The build preserves a complete Drive dialogue spine and applies a Stance frame to A1 and A3. Seed references and fingerprints make the inputs traceable; they do **not** make the resulting scene semantically approved.

## Review status

Single-card and bespoke-pair `internal-editorial-pass` means an internal model-authored rewrite with a card-specific rationale. It does not mean independent human review, blind reading or live troupe success.

Compiled pair `pair-review-pending` explicitly means the entry has not received individual semantic review. Structure, word count, hash consistency and coverage are separate from judgement about wit and coherence.

## Controls

Normal hints contain dialogue, Another scene, Done, and an optional review flag. No model installer, language-generation runtime, redundant introduction or coaching panel. General Learn to Play and actual coach exercise tools are separate from the hint.

`full`: all available examples. `nudges`: the shorter complete five-turn alternative, with ties permitting variation. `after-attempt`: examples after explicit unlock. `off`: no example controls. These saved IDs remain unchanged.

## Revision process

1. Read the exact card text and both current examples.
2. Rewrite the exchange, not just its last line.
3. Check the causal contribution of B2 and B4.
4. Check that the two alternatives have distinct situations or tactics.
5. Write the per-card rationale in the editorial source.
6. Update source bindings when seeds or instructions change.
7. Build, audit, test and review rendered mobile dialogue.
8. Ask the troupe whether the example is understandable and usable in one read. Keep independent/live status separate.
