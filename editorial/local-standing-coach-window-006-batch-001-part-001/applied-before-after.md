## D41-single-a — revised

Describe incomplete, late, or improvised work as a thoughtful version of completion.

Mechanism: D41 turns incomplete or improvised work into a supposedly thoughtful finished version.

Confusable concept: Generic positive reframing can praise a problem without claiming that unfinished work constitutes completion.

### before

**A:** Your cupboard is finished. I've chosen a handle-free design.

**B:** How do I open it?

**A:** By appreciating the uninterrupted surface first.

**B:** It won't open.

**A:** Then the storage is exceptionally secure.

### after

**A:** Your cupboard's finished. I've left the shelves loose so you can choose the heights.

**B:** They fall down. You haven't fitted the supports.

**A:** It's complete as an adjustable kit. Position everything around your tins.

**B:** I paid you to install it. Here's the drill.

**A:** Then I'll fasten one shelf at your chosen height. A finished sample, so you can approve the rest.

First / second pass: 86 / 92

- cardFidelity: original 4 — A1 calls the cupboard finished; A3 and A5 redescribe missing functionality as deliberate design. Result 5 — A1 calls loose shelves finished; A3 recasts missing supports as an adjustable kit; A5 presents one installed shelf as a finished approval sample.
- responseLogic: original 5 — B2's opening question produces A3's surface appreciation; B4's failed opening becomes A5's security claim. Result 5 — B2 identifies missing supports, causing A3's kit defense. B4 supplies a drill and insists on installation, causing A5's limited installation proposal.
- relationship: original 4 — B2 wants usable storage, and B4 tests A's claim rather than merely accepting the sales pitch. Result 4 — B4's 'I paid you to install it' establishes a customer with a credible claim and removes A3's self-assembly excuse.
- heightening: original 4 — A5 converts B4's complete failure of access into an additional supposed benefit. Result 4 — A5 turns B4's practical remedy into another incomplete deliverable framed as thoughtful completion, although actual commitment remains small.
- specificity: original 5 — A1's finished, handle-free cupboard establishes a concrete handover that B2 immediately tests. Result 5 — A1's loose cupboard shelves, B2's missing supports and B4's drill make the disputed job immediately playable.
- brevityWit: original 4 — A3's 'uninterrupted surface' and A5's 'exceptionally secure' sustain one concise design defense. Result 4 — A5's 'finished sample' economically renews A3's completion defense; the approval language is slightly polished for spontaneous speech.

B4 causes A5: B4 demands paid installation and hands over the drill; A5 offers to fasten one shelf, reframing partial installation as an approval sample.

Alternatives: Unlike D41-single-b's absent report content and executive urgency, this alternative uses a paid physical job and a customer supplying the means to finish it.

Reservation: The sample proposal postpones rather than greatly increases the consequence, but the causal chain is strong. No rewrite requested or needed for this internal pass.
