## S61-single-a — revised

Act fascinated by confident behavior; imitate it closely while resenting how naturally it seems to work.

Mechanism: A studies and closely copies B's apparently effortless confidence while begrudging B that ease.

Confusable concept: Competitive imitation alone: original A1 wants a longer pause, but A3 and A5 show admiration without the required resentment.

### before

**A:** That pause of yours made everyone listen. I'll use a longer one.

**B:** It was a cough.

**A:** An authoritative cough. Mine will be deliberate.

**B:** You don't need to copy me.

**A:** Say that again. I like the certainty.

### after

**A:** Your pause silenced the committee. I'll copy it for my report. Annoying how easily that works for you.

**B:** I was holding back a cough.

**A:** Even your cough commands respect. How long did you hold it?

**B:** Just say, 'I'd like your attention.'

**A:** I'd like your attention. Was that your exact tone? You make four words look hereditary.

First / second pass: 78 / 90

- cardFidelity: original 3 — A1 copies the pause and A5 admires certainty, but neither expresses resentment at B's effortless success. Result 5 — A1 admires and resents the commanding pause; A3 studies its duration; A5 copies B's words and envies the apparently inherited tone.
- responseLogic: original 5 — A3 converts B2's cough into authority; A5 starts copying the certainty of B4's objection. Result 5 — B2's cough becomes A3's model of authority; B4's suggested sentence becomes A5's exact rehearsal.
- relationship: original 4 — B2 corrects A's interpretation and B4 asks not to be copied, giving B an independent boundary. Result 4 — B2 punctures A's interpretation and B4 offers practical help, although B's own committee responsibility remains unspecified.
- heightening: original 4 — A5 extends imitation from B's cough to B4's attempt to stop the imitation. Result 4 — After B4 simplifies the task, A5 makes even four ordinary words evidence of B's inaccessible natural advantage.
- specificity: original 3 — A1 mentions making everyone listen but leaves the occasion and A's next speaking task unclear. Result 4 — A1 establishes a committee report and an observed pause; A5 supplies immediately playable vocal imitation.
- brevityWit: original 5 — A3's 'authoritative cough' and A5's request to repeat the objection are compact consequences of fascination. Result 4 — A3's commanding cough and A5's 'hereditary' delivery follow A's envy, though A1 states the attitude explicitly.

B4 causes A5: B4 supplies the attention request; A5 repeats it and checks the exact tone.

Alternatives: Alternative a uses a colleague's verbal coaching; b uses physical imitation while a dining companion needs to leave.

Reservation: A1 announces envy rather directly. No rewrite needed for an internal pass.

## S61-single-b — revised

Act fascinated by confident behavior; imitate it closely while resenting how naturally it seems to work.

Mechanism: A treats B's effortless confident gesture as a technique worth copying exactly and resents the effort copying costs.

Confusable concept: One-upmanship: original A5 wants to outperform B, whereas the instruction also requires fascination and close imitation.

### before

**A:** You got the table just by raising an eyebrow. Must be nice.

**B:** You could ask the waiter.

**A:** No. I'm developing a stronger eyebrow.

**B:** He's coming over.

**A:** Good. Watch mine outperform yours.

### after

**A:** You got us this table with one raised eyebrow. Show me. Must be nice having that face.

**B:** I raised the left one. But please just ask for the bill; I'm late.

**A:** Left. Like this? You barely moved yours.

**B:** He's coming. Keep it there.

**A:** You got a table in a second. I'm getting a cramp asking for paper.

First / second pass: 76 / 92

- cardFidelity: original 4 — A1's 'Must be nice' supplies resentment and A3 imitates the eyebrow, although A5 emphasizes outperforming more than close imitation. Result 5 — A1 envies B's face, A3 copies the left eyebrow precisely, and A5 resents the physical cost of reproducing B's easy authority.
- responseLogic: original 4 — A3 rejects B2's verbal request tactic; B4's approaching waiter creates the occasion for A5's demonstration. Result 5 — B2 specifies the left eyebrow and the bill; A3 follows that detail. B4 requires holding the expression, producing A5's cramp.
- relationship: original 3 — B2 offers useful advice, but B4 merely announces the waiter without giving B a clear personal stake. Result 4 — B2 wants the bill because B is late; B4 helps the attempt work while keeping A committed to it.
- heightening: original 3 — A5 promises that the eyebrow will outperform B's, largely repeating A3's stronger-eyebrow ambition. Result 4 — B4's 'Keep it there' turns A3's brief imitation into A5's painful sustained effort.
- specificity: original 5 — A1 establishes a restaurant table and an observable gesture that actors can immediately play. Result 5 — A1 establishes restaurant seating; B2 adds the bill and lateness; A3's left-eyebrow imitation is physically precise.
- brevityWit: original 4 — A3's 'developing a stronger eyebrow' is concise and follows A1's misplaced admiration. Result 4 — A5 contrasts a table gained instantly with a cramp over the bill; 'paper' is slightly less natural than the earlier wording.

B4 causes A5: B4 tells the character to sustain the eyebrow as the waiter approaches; A5 reports the resulting cramp.

Alternatives: Unlike a's workplace voice coaching, b gives B a deadline and makes imitation physically costly.

Reservation: A5's 'asking for paper' sounds shaped for the joke. No rewrite needed.
