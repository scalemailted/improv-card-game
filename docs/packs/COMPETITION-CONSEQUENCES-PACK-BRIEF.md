# Imprompt Pack Brief — Competition & Consequences

**Pack ID:** `competition-consequences`  
**Reserved IDs:** S193–S216 and D193–D216  
**Target release:** Imprompt v0.16.0  
**In-app status:** Playtest  
**Candidate pool:** 64 cards drafted; 48 selected; 8 held; 8 rejected

## Creative promise

**Competition & Consequences turns outcomes into playable human pressure.** The pack explores what people do when winning, losing, comparison, bargaining, sacrifice, and accountability shape the next choice. Its comedy comes from the difference between what a result appears to mean and what it actually costs: people define the score, raise the stakes, demand recognition, renegotiate fairness, hide how the odds changed, convert defeat into strategy, and discover that every victory creates a next obligation.

A card belongs when it gives the holder a clear relationship to a contest, bargain, comparison, or consequence while remaining portable across settings. The “competition” may be explicit, private, emotional, professional, ceremonial, domestic, imaginary, or invented during the scene.

## Central comic question

> **What happens when winning is no longer enough—and someone must decide what the result means, who pays for it, and what happens next?**

The card gives one performer a private lens or engine. It never determines the partner’s score, willingness to compete, reaction to losing, or acceptance of the consequences.

## What makes this pack distinct

- **Core Foundations** provides broad scene fundamentals.
- **Everyday Friction** asks why a small inconvenience has become personal.
- **Power Games** asks who gets to decide and what makes authority real.
- **Relationship Knots** asks what remains unresolved between people.
- **Emotional Pressure** asks which feeling reorganizes behavior.
- **Secrets & Schemes** asks how information is hidden, pursued, and traded.
- **Absurd Commitment** asks how an impossible premise becomes coherent and consequential.
- **Rules, Rituals & Institutions** asks how people use, preserve, interpret, resist, or hide inside systems.
- **Competition & Consequences** asks what counts as winning, what the stakes demand, and who owns the result.

A Power Games card is primarily about rank or leverage. A Competition & Consequences card is primarily about comparison, outcome, exchange, sacrifice, responsibility, or the price attached to success—even when the result also changes status.

## Included territory

- explicit and unspoken competitions;
- rivalry, scorekeeping, comparison, and performance;
- prizes, penalties, bargains, concessions, and tradeoffs;
- fairness, legitimacy, judging, and disputed outcomes;
- teamwork under unequal rewards or risks;
- victory, defeat, comeback, rematch, and escalation;
- sacrifice, contribution, responsibility, and accountability;
- people hiding how they changed the odds or softened the challenge;
- practical questions about who benefits, who pays, and what changes next;
- emotional investment in being chosen, recognized, or allowed to matter.

## Excluded territory

This pack should not rely on:

- humiliation, bullying, or fixed loser identities as the joke;
- real-money gambling pressure or instructions for illegal betting;
- specific cheating tactics or harmful real-world manipulation;
- guaranteeing that another performer wins, loses, concedes, or accepts a penalty;
- declaring another performer’s private ambition, jealousy, or willingness to compete;
- fixed sports, game-show, courtroom, business, or tournament premises;
- pure authority contests better suited to Power Games;
- task fairness better suited to Everyday Friction;
- relationship rivalry without meaningful stakes or consequences;
- escalation that produces novelty without preserving the scene’s established logic.

## Portability rule

The prompt defines a relationship to stakes and outcomes, not a specific contest.

Too narrow:

> You are the defending champion in a televised cooking tournament.

Portable:

> **Winner Sets the Terms** — Carry yourself as though success gives you the right to define the next challenge, the reward, and what counts as fair.

The portable version can function between coworkers, siblings, monarchs, adventurers, neighbors, artists, athletes, ghosts, robots, or two people deciding who gets the last chair.

## Hidden-information rule

A Stance may define how the holder measures success, interprets comparison, experiences risk, or relates to consequences. It cannot declare that another performer is competitive, defeated, jealous, guilty, or impressed.

A Drive may seek a concession, recognition, bargain, choice, confession, or acceptance of consequences. Resistance must create more play rather than invalidate the prompt.

Weak:

> The other performer knows you won and must admit they are inferior.

Strong:

> **Call It a Fair Win** — Get them to recognize the result as deserved rather than accidental, gifted, or manipulated.

## Desired player experience

A player should quickly understand:

1. what outcome, comparison, bargain, or consequence matters to the holder;
2. the first observable behavior the card creates;
3. how to respond if the partner refuses the contest or disputes the stakes;
4. what score, term, cost, or recurring behavior can heighten;
5. how success and failure can both generate more scene;
6. how to compete intensely without blocking collaborative discovery.

## Exercise value

Competition & Consequences materially expands:

- **Status Clash**, through winners defining terms, precarious standing, judging, scoring, and disputed legitimacy;
- **Crown & Heart**, through recognition, worth, sacrifice, belonging, and the emotional price of victory;
- **Past & Future**, through rematches, old concessions, shared competition, and unpaid consequences;
- **Pursuer & Avoider**, through bargains, scorekeeping, confession, concealed odds, and responsibility;
- **Absurd Commitment**, through ceremonial winners, sudden-death choices, literal stakes, and consequences that compound;
- custom exercises centered on rivalry, teamwork, negotiation, accountability, sacrifice, comeback, or consequence.

## Live-playtest questions

1. Do players make the stakes visible through behavior rather than explaining a fictional scoreboard?
2. Can every card function when the scene contains no literal contest?
3. Do bargains and consequences deepen relationships rather than turning the scene into negotiation-only dialogue?
4. Can partners refuse to compete without making the holder’s card unusable?
5. Are scorekeeping cards distinct from general rivalry and status behavior?
6. Do accountability cards create forward action instead of moral lectures?
7. Are high-intensity cards playable without shouting or hostility?
8. Are any cards too close to Everything Is a Contest, Old Rivals, Keeping Score, Rank Everything, or Fair Exchange?
9. Does the pack deepen Status Clash while remaining distinct from Power Games?
10. Which prompts generate satisfying postmortems about what success cost and who owned the result?
