## S236-single-a — revised

Choose one early detail as the warning sign, then let every later development reveal a larger version of the same disaster.

Mechanism: A reads each widening structural failure as a larger expression of the same early crack's warning.

Confusable concept: Saying 'I told you so' vindicates an earlier opinion; this card repeatedly scales one early detail into the developing disaster.

### before

**A:** I said that tiny crack in the stage was a warning.

**B:** Now the piano's leaning.

**A:** The crack has learned to accompany music.

**B:** We need to stop the concert.

**A:** Start by listening to the stage. It auditioned its objection this morning.

### after

**A:** That little crack beside the piano was the stage warning us.

**B:** The piano's leaning. Help me move it.

**A:** The crack's opened a place for the whole piano. Keep your end away from it.

**B:** The front row's tilting too. I'm clearing the hall.

**A:** It's reached the audience. That little crack is taking the whole concert downstairs. I'll get the balcony.

First / second pass: 59 / 95

- cardFidelity: original 3 — A1 selects the crack and A3 enlarges it through the piano, but A5 returns to the initial warning without a larger disaster. Result 5 — A1 names the small crack as warning; A3 expands it to the piano; A5 treats the threatened audience and concert as its larger disaster.
- responseLogic: original 3 — A3 uses B2's leaning piano; A5's instruction to listen barely engages B4's need to stop the concert. Result 5 — B2's leaning piano informs A3's moving advice; B4's tilting front row and evacuation lead A5 to take responsibility for the balcony.
- relationship: original 3 — B4 wants an urgent intervention, while A5 offers a retrospective rebuke rather than a developed response. Result 4 — B2 recruits practical help and B4 initiates clearing the hall; A3 and A5 cooperate while interpreting the expanding warning.
- heightening: original 2 — A5 revisits what happened 'this morning' instead of expanding the consequence established by B2. Result 5 — A5 expands the crack's consequence from an instrument to the whole concert and adds an evacuation task prompted by B4.
- specificity: original 4 — A1's stage crack and B2's leaning piano establish a concrete concert problem. Result 5 — A1 locates the crack beside the piano; B2 and B4 establish concrete physical deterioration and playable urgent tasks.
- brevityWit: original 3 — A3's musical personification is apt, but A5's 'auditioned its objection' repeats ornament instead of developing the situation. Result 4 — A5's concert going 'downstairs' follows the structural threat and A1's personified warning, though the danger dominates the humor.

B4 causes A5: B4 expands the danger to seating and begins evacuation; A5 interprets the concert-wide disaster and takes the balcony.

Alternatives: Alternative a pairs literal expanding danger with cooperative action; b maps escalating administrative errors onto a metaphorical spreading typo.

Reservation: No rewrite needed. Because the warning is physically accurate, actors have less interpretive eccentricity to play than in the wedding alternative.

## S236-single-b — revised

Choose one early detail as the warning sign, then let every later development reveal a larger version of the same disaster.

Mechanism: A selects the invitation error as an early warning and reads the cake's wrong names and competing room entitlement as larger versions of that identity disaster.

Confusable concept: Accumulating unrelated wedding mishaps supplies escalating inconvenience; this card makes A repeatedly interpret each development through the same original naming warning.

### before

**A:** The first wedding invitation came back misspelled. That was the warning.

**B:** Now the cake names the wrong couple.

**A:** Same mistake, larger icing budget.

**B:** The venue has two weddings booked.

**A:** There it is. The typo has acquired a building.

### after

**A:** They misspelled our wedding invitation. That was the warning.

**B:** The cake names another couple. Help me scrape it off.

**A:** Same mistake, larger icing budget. Pass me a knife.

**B:** They booked that couple into our reception room too. We've both paid; I'm offering half.

**A:** The typo's taking half our wedding. Before you offer them my chair, check which name's on it.

First / second pass: 90 / 86

- cardFidelity: original 5 — A1 selects a misspelled invitation; A3 and A5 read cake and venue confusion as enlargements of that error. Result 4 — A1 names the invitation misspelling as a warning; A3 reads the cake error as the same mistake at greater expense; A5 sees the reception conflict as the typo taking half the wedding. The link is interpretive rather than literal.
- responseLogic: original 4 — A3 uses B2's wrong names and A5 uses B4's double booking, although misspelling and duplicate booking differ literally. Result 5 — B2 asks for help scraping names off the cake, and A3 requests a knife. B4 offers half the room to the other paying couple, and A5 explicitly reacts to losing half the wedding and worries about the chair next.
- relationship: original 3 — B2 and B4 bring consequential wedding updates but establish little personal objective beyond reporting them. Result 4 — B2 pursues a practical cake repair, while B4 negotiates shared space because both couples paid. These solutions give A3 and A5 concrete developments to reinterpret without reducing B to agreement.
- heightening: original 5 — A5 expands the naming error from invitation to cake to an entire building after B4's double-booking news. Result 4 — B4 escalates from removable icing to a competing claim on the paid reception room. A5 converts B's compromise into the warning's larger fulfillment, then anticipates losing A's own chair.
- specificity: original 5 — A1's invitation, B2's cake names and B4's venue booking create an immediately playable wedding crisis. Result 5 — A1 establishes a wedding invitation error naturally. B2's cake inscription and B4's double-booked reception provide immediately playable objects, stakes and practical choices.
- brevityWit: original 5 — A3's 'larger icing budget' and A5's typo acquiring a building compress the same escalating interpretation. Result 4 — A3's 'larger icing budget' compresses the escalating-error logic. A5's chair warning follows B4's offer of half the room, though it is a smaller comic image than the reception loss.

B4 causes A5: B4 reveals a reception booking conflict and proposes giving up half. A5 adopts that exact fraction as evidence of the expanding disaster and challenges further concessions through the chair warning.

Alternatives: The preserved alternative follows physical structural failure and cooperative evacuation. This candidate follows mistaken wedding identity while B repairs and bargains; the helpful compromise itself becomes A's next evidence of disaster.

Reservation: A misspelling, another couple's cake and a booking conflict are not literally one error; A's sincere interpretation supplies their unity. The final chair image adds specificity more than scale. These are minor reservations; no rewrite is needed.
