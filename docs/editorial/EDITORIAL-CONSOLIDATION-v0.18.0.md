# Imprompt v0.18.0 — Editorial Consolidation

**Release purpose:** convert the complete 480-card construction library into a controlled live-playtest library by resolving the strongest semantic collisions, broadening narrow titles, preserving stable IDs, and recording every copy change.

## Outcome

| Measure | Result |
|---|---:|
| Active library | 240 Stances + 240 Drives |
| Strong semantic rewrites | 24 |
| Generalized titles | 4 |
| Total revised cards | 28 |
| High-priority overlap clusters addressed | 23 of 23 |
| Full subtheme matrix internally live-validation-ready | 48 of 48 |
| Stable card IDs preserved | 28 of 28 |
| Revised cards carrying `contentVersion: 0.18.0` | 28 of 28 |

Every cluster remains subject to live troupe validation. “Resolved” in this document means that the revised cards now have materially different first moves, escalation paths, interaction patterns, or consequences under internal editorial review; it is not a claim that external playtesting is complete.

## Semantic revisions

| ID | Pack | Previous card | v0.18.0 card | Distinctive engine after revision |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| D60 | Power Games | **Yield Control** — Make someone surrender control of a decision, resource, or symbol of authority. | **Pass Me the Authority** — Get someone to transfer authority publicly by naming you as the person who decides what happens next. | Requires a visible transfer of legitimacy instead of another surrender of a task, object, or decision. |
| D127 | Secrets & Schemes | **Join the Inner Circle** — Recruit someone into a hidden plan by offering trust, purpose, and just enough privileged information. | **Back My Version** — Recruit someone to remember, repeat, and defend one shared version of events when questions begin. | Recruits a specific witness and story-keeping function rather than another general partner in a secret plan. |
| D133 | Secrets & Schemes | **The Helpful Suspect** — Hide your role in creating the problem while eagerly organizing the investigation. | **Steer the Suspicion** — Hide your responsibility by redirecting every new clue toward a safer explanation before anyone can connect it back to you. | Turns culpability into active evidence control rather than enthusiastic participation in the investigation. |
| D136 | Secrets & Schemes | **Confess Before I Do** — Maneuver someone into admitting their secret, motive, or mistake before you reveal your own. | **Tell Me Why** — Get someone to reveal the motive behind a choice by offering small pieces of your own truth as bait. | Seeks motive through reciprocal partial disclosure instead of simply forcing the other person to confess first. |
| D155 | Absurd Commitment | **Prove You Accept the Premise** — Create an increasingly inconvenient test of whether someone will act as though the impossible premise is true. | **Act as If It’s True** — Ask for one concrete choice that would only make sense if the impossible premise were true. | Tests belief through one meaningful consequence rather than escalating inconvenience as proof of obedience. |
| D159 | Absurd Commitment | **A Kinder Impossible Truth** — Protect someone from a harsher explanation by maintaining a more hopeful and impossible version of events. | **Keep Hope Operational** — Protect their hope with practical evidence and next steps while quietly withholding what makes the impossible outcome unlikely. | Sustains hope through practical action and selective evidence rather than offering another kinder version of the truth. |
| D161 | Absurd Commitment | **That Was Phase One** — Treat every failed attempt as a necessary stage that proves the impossible plan is advancing. | **Failure Reveals the Mission** — Use each failed step to reveal a different goal the plan was secretly pursuing, then act immediately on that new mission. | Makes failure change the active goal instead of claiming every failure was merely the next planned phase. |
| D170 | Rules, Rituals & Institutions | **You Haven’t Been Dismissed** — Keep someone present by insisting that one final step, acknowledgment, or ceremony must happen before anyone may leave. | **Close It Properly** — Keep them present until both of you complete the ritual that formally ends this role, meeting, or relationship. | Pursues mutual completion of a transition instead of treating departure as permission the holder controls. |
| D179 | Rules, Rituals & Institutions | **Follow the Custom** — Create increasingly inconvenient formalities to test whether someone respects the group, relationship, or institution. | **Interpret the Custom** — Ask them to apply an ambiguous custom where the wording is unclear. Treat their interpretation as the real test of loyalty. | Tests interpretive values and respect for a custom rather than adding increasingly inconvenient formalities. |
| D211 | Competition & Consequences | **Turn It into a Tiebreaker** — Turn every disagreement into another round designed to settle who is better, right, or more committed. | **No Tie Stands** — Turn every even outcome into a formal rematch with a new rule and a consequence for losing. | Creates a recurring rematch protocol around ties rather than turning every disagreement into a generic contest. |
| D218 | Advanced Scene Engines | **One More Unresolved Reason** — Keep them present by introducing a new unresolved reason whenever the previous reason is answered. | **Reopen the First Reason** — Keep them present by returning to the first reason for staying and revealing that it now means something different. | Recontextualizes an earlier reason instead of continually inventing one more reason to stay. |
| S53 | Power Games | **I Made You Ready** — Treat another person’s competence as evidence of your teaching, influence, and continuing seniority. | **The Crown Must Pass** — Treat another person as your likely successor. Prepare them to lead, then reclaim authority whenever their independence makes your influence feel temporary. | Reframes mentorship as ambivalence about succession rather than claiming credit for another person’s competence. |
| S77 | Relationship Knots | **Older in This Relationship** — Treat your longer memory of the relationship as seniority, correcting how its history should be understood. | **Keeper of Precedent** — Treat your longer memory as an archive, not a throne. Cite specific promises and precedents whenever the relationship faces a new choice. | Uses specific precedent as guidance instead of claiming superior authority to interpret the entire relationship. |
| S83 | Relationship Knots | **My First Call** — Turn to this connection first whenever uncertainty appears, then minimize how much that instinct reveals about your dependence. | **Old Reflex** — Turn to this person first whenever uncertainty appears, as an old reflex. Notice the habit only after you have already involved them. | Centers involuntary relationship habit rather than exclusive trust and concealed dependence. |
| S108 | Emotional Pressure | **The Feeling Never Left** — Treat an old hurt as emotionally current, responding to new offers as though part of that moment is happening again. | **Careful Around the Scar** — Treat one harmless detail as the trigger for an old hurt. Become overly careful, helpful, or controlled whenever it returns. | Adds a concrete recurring trigger and coping pattern instead of keeping all old hurt generically current. |
| S144 | Secrets & Schemes | **Everything Connects** — Treat every interruption, coincidence, and denial as part of one expanding hidden design, and doubt as further evidence. | **Competing Theories** — Create several explanations for what is happening. Test each new clue against them, and switch allegiance whenever one theory gains strength. | Replaces one unquestionable grand design with active comparison among rival explanations. |
| S161 | Absurd Commitment | **I Get the Joke** — Act as though immediate acceptance of every strange development is the price of belonging, even when you are visibly struggling. | **Half a Beat Late** — Use the group’s reactions as your cue, joining each one a moment late and escalating your enthusiasm to stay included. | Creates a social-timing pattern instead of another attempt to prove belonging by accepting the bizarre premise. |
| S172 | Rules, Rituals & Institutions | **Knows the Customs** — Behave as though effortless knowledge of the unwritten rules proves you belong. Follow them conspicuously and conceal every moment of uncertainty. | **Customs Interpreter** — Act as the translator of unwritten customs. Correct breaches, explain exceptions, and make your usefulness proof that you belong. | Establishes belonging through useful interpretation rather than another performance of insider familiarity. |
| S179 | Rules, Rituals & Institutions | **I Need Your Access** — Use someone’s guidance to navigate the system while resisting every sign that their help gives them influence over you. | **My Sponsor Inside** — Treat one person as your sponsor inside the system. Seek their introductions and approval while resisting the identity their support assigns you. | Centers sponsorship and identity costs instead of relying on another person to explain how the system works. |
| S202 | Competition & Consequences | **Permanent Scoreboard** — Treat every offer, memory, and achievement as another point in a rivalry that has never truly ended. | **Debts on the Scoreboard** — Treat past wins and losses as debts. Let each new result change who owes the next favor, risk, or concession. | Makes remembered scores alter present obligations instead of merely repeating a general rivalry. |
| S203 | Competition & Consequences | **The Rival I Need** — Treat one person as essential to sharpening your effort, while resisting every sign that your ambition depends on their presence. | **Victory Without You** — Treat the possibility of winning without this rival as strangely hollow. Keep raising the challenge so the relationship survives the result. | Makes the feared end of the rivalry the engine rather than dependence on the rival’s judgment. |
| S208 | Competition & Consequences | **I Decide What You Can Risk** — Frame control over someone else’s choices as protection from consequences you believe they may underestimate. | **Your Risk, My Cost** — Treat every choice around you as a risk whose consequences may land on you. Press for a voice in decisions you cannot control. | Shifts from protective paternalism to unequal exposure to consequences and a demand for shared consent. |
| S218 | Advanced Scene Engines | **One Detail Could Undo Me** — Project total control while treating one recurring detail as the thing that could expose how little authority you truly possess. | **Authority Changes Hands** — Project control while guarding one detail that could transfer authority elsewhere. Let each return of that detail shift whom you defer to or overrule. | Makes the recurring detail actively reverse authority rather than simply expose a bluff. |
| S240 | Advanced Scene Engines | **Everything Rhymes Eventually** — Treat repeated words, actions, and reversals as echoes of one larger pattern whose meaning you keep revising. | **The Callback Changes Meaning** — Treat every returning phrase or action as evidence the relationship has changed. Repeat it with a new emotional meaning each time. | Uses callbacks to track evolving relationship meaning rather than building another cosmic pattern. |

## Stable identity and historical logs

- Card IDs were not renumbered or recycled.
- Only cards whose visible copy changed received `contentVersion: "0.18.0"`.
- Unchanged cards retain the content version of the release in which their current wording was introduced.
- Pack-level versions advance to `0.18.0` because each playtest pack participated in the editorial-readiness pass.
- Completed Scene Log entries retain immutable `stanceSnapshot` and `driveSnapshot` objects. Opening an older log therefore shows the wording actually played, not the revised live-library copy.
