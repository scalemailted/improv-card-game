4. THE PRODUCT AND THE ACTUAL WRITING FAILURE
============================================================

Imprompt is a smartphone-first, client-side improv card game.

Each performer privately draws:
- STANCE: how the character interprets and reacts to the situation.
- DRIVE: what the character pursues, protects, avoids, or repeats.

A single-card example demonstrates that exact instruction.
A combination example has A hold BOTH selected cards.

In a combination, the Stance must change HOW A pursues the Drive.
Mentioning one concept and then making an unrelated request from
the other is not a fusion.

The stored hints are short performed dialogue, not coaching prose.
They must give the player an immediately understandable illustration.

Human readers report that many current examples:
- Lack enough context to understand the interaction.
- Jump between unrelated ideas.
- Introduce facts, conditions, or stakes without preparing them.
- Give B implausible lines merely to set up A.
- End with a clever-sounding non sequitur.
- Need the reader to repair the scene mentally.

Your task is to fix those actual failures, not write explanations
that make the intended meaning sound plausible.

CORE STANDARD:

First:
“Does this conversation make sense from the words on the screen?”

Then:
“Does A's particular way of handling this situation demonstrate the
exact card, or causally merge the exact Stance and Drive?”

The character may be irrational.
The writer's presentation must remain understandable.

5. REQUIRED DIALOGUE FORM
============================================================

Use five SPOKEN turns in A–B–A–B–A order.

No separate narrator paragraphs, gray action blocks, stage directions,
instructional introductions, or coaching explanations in the hint.

A1 — Establish the immediate interaction through natural speech.
A reader should begin to understand what is happening.

B2 — Respond credibly to A1.
Contribute a want, fact, limitation, offer, correction, or objection.

A3 — Use B2.
Reveal the exact card-driven interpretation or tactic.

B4 — Respond to what A actually did.
Change the pressure, available options, or understanding.

A5 — Use B4 to develop the SAME character behavior.
The final beat must be earned by this exchange.

Do not mechanically fill these five positions with stock lines.
They describe conversational functions, not a sentence template.

Context requirements:
- Establish what the two people are doing.
- Establish why the immediate issue matters enough to discuss.
- Make the relevant relationship or authority understandable when
  the exchange depends on it.
- Keep ownership, timing, knowledge, and material facts coherent.

Do not require unnecessary biographies, names, or explicit locations.
“You're the headteacher” matters in a rank dispute.
The headteacher's full name and school address do not.

A small stake is sufficient: finishing lunch, being appreciated,
avoiding embarrassment, keeping company, gaining access, or
delegating an awkward announcement.

Brevity:
Use the repository's actual supported word limits.
Do not omit essential context merely to reduce the word count.
Do not pad the exchange to the maximum.
Do not silently truncate a longer draft.

If a technical limit demonstrably prevents a clear example, record
the specific conflict. Do not falsify a length check.

6. COACH-2.0 — MANDATORY ACCEPTANCE GATES
============================================================

Every gate receives:
PASS, FAIL, or UNCLEAR, with concise scene-specific evidence.

UNCLEAR is not acceptance.
No numerical score compensates for a failed mandatory gate.

G1 — LEGIBLE SITUATION AND STAKES

Can the reader understand the immediate interaction without the
card description, author's rationale, or invented backstory?

Distinguish:
- Stated facts: identify the supplying turn.
- Reasonable ordinary inference: identify supporting wording.
- Unestablished essentials: mark missing.

Harmless ambiguity is allowed.
Missing information needed to understand a response is not.

Identify the first confusing turn rather than vaguely requesting
“more context.”

G2 — COHERENT FACTS, LANGUAGE, AND KNOWLEDGE

Track material objects, decisions, ownership, timing, responsibilities,
and who knows what.

They must remain coherent unless a recognizable lie, correction,
revelation, or misunderstanding changes the understanding.

Reject:
- Unexplained object or setting changes.
- An abstract phrase treated as a physical object by accident.
- A suddenly invented authority, deadline, payment, or agreement
  required only to make the ending work.
- Knowledge a character has no intelligible way to possess.
- A response that depends on an unstated intervening event.

An unreasonable moral argument may be the comedy.
An incomprehensible transition is not character comedy.

G3 — ALL FOUR CONVERSATIONAL TRANSITIONS CONNECT

Check:
A1→B2
B2→A3
A3→B4
B4→A5

For each transition identify what is being answered, used, resisted,
reinterpreted, or developed.

Repeated vocabulary alone does not establish a connection.

Deliberate avoidance is allowed when it reads as purposeful behavior.
It must not be indistinguishable from the writer changing subjects.

A3 must use something B2 supplied.
A5 must use something B4 supplied.

G4 — B IS A PLAUSIBLE PARTICIPANT

B has a credible position, practical aim, emotional response,
constraint, or relationship to the issue.

Ask:
“Would B plausibly say this here without knowing that the writer
needs a setup for A's final joke?”

B may cooperate, help, bargain, concede, correct, reveal, or resist.
Do not turn every scene into a refusal contest.

B must affect A, not merely cue A's prepared speech.

G5 — THE EXACT CARD CAUSES A'S CHOICES

For a single:
Identify the card's distinguishing mechanism and show where it
changes A's interpretation, pursuit, reaction, or tactic.

A generic mood or broad category resemblance is insufficient.
A Drive need not be accomplished; its pursuit must be visible.

For a pair:
Identify:
- What comes from the Stance.
- What comes from the Drive.
- How the Stance changes A's means of pursuing the Drive.
- How B responds to that combined behavior.

If one card is merely decorative, the pair fails.

Diagnostic:
Would removing a defining instruction remove an important reason
for this tactic or reaction?

Do not demand that no other card could fit. Some cards overlap.
Demand a real contribution from the selected instruction(s).

A holds the selected cards.
B's dialogue is an illustrative response, not an instruction
imposed on the real scene partner.

G6 — THE ENDING GROWS FROM THIS EXCHANGE

A5 develops the established behavior using B4.

Heightening may be:
- Accepting a solution on revealing terms.
- Turning a concession into leverage.
- Deepening vulnerability.
- Shifting status.
- Exposing the cost of an earlier choice.
- Following an absurd premise to an understandable consequence.

It need not be louder, crueler, stranger, or more obstructive.

Reject an unrelated punchline or an unprepared new fact that
conveniently rescues the ending.

Ask whether A5 would work equally well after an unrelated B4.
If so, investigate whether the ending is detachable.

FORMAT GATE

Five spoken ABABA turns.
Supported schema and word limit.
No narrator, coaching, or stage-direction block.
No accidental sixth turn.
No silently truncated dialogue.

7. WEIGHTED RUBRIC AFTER THE GATES
============================================================

Preserve these six weights:

Card fidelity                     30
Response logic                    20
Relationship and interaction      15
Heightening                       15
Specificity and playability       10
Brevity and earned wit            10

Rate each dimension 0–5:

0: absent or contradictory.
1: major failure.
2: weak.
3: workable but unpolished.
4: strong with minor reservations.
5: unusually clear and effective.

Calculate the total in code:
sum(weight × rating / 5).

Acceptance requires:
- Every mandatory gate passes.
- Total at least 85/100.
- Card fidelity at least 4/5.
- Response logic at least 4/5.
- Every other dimension at least 3/5.

Do not award 5 merely because a feature is present.
Do not round 84 up to a pass.
Do not inflate scores to finish a batch.

A complete record is not evidence of a good scene.
The judgments must refer to actual dialogue.

WIT AND VOICE

Do not settle for merely grammatical, on-topic exchanges.

Aim for humor and recognition arising from:
- The character's sincere priorities.
- A revealing choice.
- A response to the partner's offer.
- An earned reversal.
- An ordinary consequence taken personally or unusually seriously.

Prefer natural spoken wording over explanatory slogans.

Do not default to:
- Paperwork and hierarchy for unrelated cards.
- Smugness, sarcasm, or hostility.
- Arbitrary supernatural details.
- An obscure pun that requires explanation.
- The same argument with different nouns.
- B creating a contrived problem purely to feed a punchline.

Never claim the numerical rubric objectively guarantees a laugh.
Human/live approval remains separate.

8. QUALITY REFERENCE — PRESERVE VERBATIM
============================================================

S67 — Chain of Command:
Treat every question, favor, and decision as something that must
travel through the proper hierarchy.

D101 — Start the Hard Part:
Get someone else to explain, announce, or begin the emotionally
difficult part.

Owner-endorsed example, S67+D101-a:

A: You're the headteacher. You should tell the parents I lost
   the class hamster.
B: You're its teacher. You tell them.
A: I can supply the facts. Bad news should come from a senior
   position.
B: Fine. I'll appoint you deputy head for the meeting.
A: My first recommendation is that we hear from the headteacher.

Why this works:
- A1 establishes the problem, responsibility, and relevant hierarchy.
- B2 challenges A's attempted delegation.
- A3 uses hierarchy to preserve that delegation.
- B4 changes A's rank to defeat the excuse.
- A5 uses the newly granted rank to route the announcement upward.

Both cards drive one tactic.
The last line needs B's intervention.

Preserve this exact example. Record any supplemental review without
rewriting the endorsed dialogue.

Learn its causal responsiveness, not its surface formula.
Do not fill the library with promotions, committees, or delegation.
