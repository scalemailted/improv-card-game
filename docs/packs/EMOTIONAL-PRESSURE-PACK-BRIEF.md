# Imprompt Pack Brief — Emotional Pressure

**Pack ID:** `emotional-pressure`  
**Reserved IDs:** S97–S120 and D97–D120  
**Target release:** Imprompt v0.12.0  
**In-app status:** Playtest  
**Candidate pool:** 64 cards drafted; 48 selected; 8 held; 8 rejected

## Creative promise

**Emotional Pressure turns insecurity, validation, resentment, hope, grief, shame, and uncertainty into visible choices that can repeat and heighten.** The pack does not ask performers merely to “be sad,” “be angry,” or “be afraid.” Each card converts an emotional condition into a point of view, objective, concealment strategy, ritual, or recurring behavior.

A card belongs when the holder can show emotional stakes through action: controlling the room through disappointment, measuring safety through reassurance, performing strength, preserving hope against evidence, translating feelings into practical care, competing over whose pain counts, or making every new detail confirm a feared ending.

## Central comic question

> **What do I do when the feeling is larger than the situation—and I still need another person to respond?**

The pressure may come from hope, dread, grief, shame, resentment, longing, pride, or the need to be reassured. The cards leave the source of that feeling open so performers can discover the relationship and circumstances together.

## What makes this pack distinct

Core Foundations supplies broad emotional premises. Everyday Friction attaches pressure to ordinary responsibilities. Power Games asks who gets to decide. Relationship Knots explores what a connection means. Emotional Pressure focuses on **how a feeling reorganizes the holder’s behavior**:

- composure can become authority, performance, or dependency;
- reassurance may be sought indirectly, repeatedly, or through tests;
- disappointment can become influence, rivalry, evidence, or a demand for recognition;
- grief can create ritual, seniority, unfinished time, or practical care;
- hope can become resilience, denial, recruitment, or a grand theory of the future;
- shame can produce editing, pride, deflection, imitation, or resistance to pity;
- emotional labor can become service, leverage, avoidance, competition, or debt;
- fear can be translated into control, preparation, interrogation, or catastrophe.

## Included territory

- insecurity, uncertainty, rejection sensitivity, and threatened self-worth;
- the need for validation, reassurance, recognition, acceptance, or permission to feel;
- resentment, disappointment, guilt, shame, and emotional comparison;
- grief, loss, longing, memory, and the pressure to keep functioning;
- hope, optimism, resilience, denial, and the refusal to surrender possibility;
- emotional care, comfort, buffering, and intervention;
- composure, strength, vulnerability, and the performance of readiness;
- rituals, practical responses, fixations, and grand meanings attached to feelings;
- emotional contradictions such as comforting others while becoming less composed;
- objectives that seek acknowledgment, safety, truth, presence, or emotional risk.

## Excluded territory

This pack should not rely on:

- diagnostic labels or mental-health conditions as character shorthand;
- instructions to imitate panic attacks, depression, trauma responses, or other conditions;
- forced crying, physical affection, disclosure, or emotional breakdown;
- a declaration of another performer’s private emotion or future reaction;
- generic “be sad,” “be angry,” or “be emotional” prompts without behavior;
- a fixed bereavement, breakup, therapy, hospital, or crisis premise;
- trauma disclosure as entertainment or proof of commitment;
- relationship-definition material whose main question belongs in Relationship Knots;
- manipulation or concealment whose main engine belongs in Secrets & Schemes;
- victory or punishment structures whose main engine belongs in Competition & Consequences.

## Portability rule

The card describes how the holder manages pressure, not the event that caused it.

Too narrow:

> Your parent died yesterday, and you must organize the funeral.

Portable:

> **The Proper Way to Grieve** — Treat difficult feelings as requiring a precise ritual, sequence, or ceremony, and correct any attempt to rush or simplify it.

The portable version can work around bereavement, a failed audition, a lost object, an ending tradition, a vanished kingdom, or an absurdly disappointing sandwich.

## Hidden-information rule

A Stance may define what the holder fears, hopes, notices, or interprets. It cannot declare another performer’s feelings or guarantee comfort, rejection, forgiveness, or approval.

Weak:

> They are about to reject you.

Strong:

> **Waiting for the Bad News** — Treat pauses, changes, and uncertainty as signs that rejection or loss is approaching, then prepare yourself before it arrives.

A Drive may pursue reassurance, acknowledgment, honesty, presence, or vulnerability, but it must remain playable when the partner resists or responds unexpectedly.

## Desired player experience

A player should quickly understand:

1. what emotional evidence the character is watching for;
2. the first visible behavior created by that pressure;
3. what the behavior protects, seeks, or avoids;
4. how the pattern can recur without turning into a single volume or facial expression;
5. how the card remains responsive to the partner rather than becoming a private monologue.

The best cards invite contrast. A performer may be internally frightened while externally practical, deeply hopeful while collecting bad evidence, or desperate for reassurance while refusing pity.

## Metadata additions

This pack adds six reusable coach roles:

`griever`, `hope-keeper`, `reassurance-seeker`, `shame-bearer`, `emotional-buffer`, and `comforter`.

It also adds controlled motifs:

`anxiety`, `comfort`, `courage`, `disappointment`, `dread`, `grief`, `hope`, `longing`, `reassurance`, `relief`, `resilience`, `shame`, `uncertainty`, `vulnerability`, and `worth`.

These remain internal metadata. The visible seven-category system does not change.

## Exercise value

Emotional Pressure materially deepens existing coach presets:

- **Emotional Stakes** receives six new Emotional Assumptions Stances centered on courage, hope, dread, care, reassurance, and pride;
- **Crown & Heart** gains stronger emotional dependence, validation, shame, and recognition material for its Heart assignment;
- **Relationship Pressure** gains grief, reassurance, resentment, and protective concealment without fixing a relationship label;
- **Past & Future** gains remembered hope, emotional debt, strong-one roles, and unfinished hurt;
- custom exercises can use roles such as Hope Keeper, Reassurance Seeker, Shame Bearer, Emotional Buffer, Comforter, and Griever.

## Live-playtest questions

1. Can a player express the card through choices rather than increasing emotional volume?
2. Does the prompt create interaction rather than introspection or monologue?
3. Do high-intensity cards remain safe and playable for beginners?
4. Are grief and shame prompts broad enough to avoid forcing personal material?
5. Does hope create active behavior rather than passive optimism?
6. Do reassurance objectives remain playable when the partner refuses to reassure?
7. Are any cards too close to Relationship Knots or Core Foundations?
8. Do Emotional Stakes and Crown & Heart now produce noticeably greater variety?
9. Which cards are vetoed because they are unclear versus simply challenging?
10. Can performers heighten the behavior while keeping the underlying emotion truthful?
