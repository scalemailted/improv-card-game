Model review, not human approval or live testing.

## S99-single-a — revised

Treat every reaction as evidence you can expertly interpret; translate feelings, correct emotional misunderstandings, and prescribe the proper response.

Mechanism: A treats B's reactions as expert-readable evidence, overrides B's explanation and prescribes how B should express the diagnosed feeling.

Confusable concept: Merely naming emotions would stop at diagnosis; the exact card also corrects interpretation and dictates a proper response.

### before

**A:** You said fine while folding your napkin. That's guarded resentment.

**B:** It means the napkin was untidy.

**A:** Order as emotional protection. Very clear.

**B:** Stop analysing me.

**A:** Excellent. We've found a feeling with a proper name.

### after

**A:** You said dinner was fine while folding your napkin. That's guarded resentment.

**B:** It means the napkin was untidy.

**A:** You're restoring order because you feel unheard. Tell me what you needed from dinner.

**B:** To eat without being analysed.

**A:** Good boundary. Now say it without hiding behind the napkin, so I can acknowledge it properly.

First / second pass: 74 / 95

- cardFidelity: original 4 — A1 diagnoses resentment and A3 corrects B's account, but A5 never prescribes the proper response. Result 5 — A1 diagnoses napkin folding, A3 corrects B's literal explanation, and A5 prescribes how B must express a boundary.
- responseLogic: original 4 — A3 recasts B2's tidiness as protection; A5 treats B4's objection as diagnostic success. Result 5 — B2's tidiness becomes A3's emotional order-restoration; B4's request to avoid analysis becomes A5's coached boundary.
- relationship: original 3 — B4 clearly wants the analysis stopped, but A5 provides another label without changing the interaction. Result 4 — B2 wants an ordinary action understood literally and B4 explicitly wants an unanalysed meal, giving A a credible opposing need.
- heightening: original 3 — A5 congratulates the process rather than moving from interpretation to an imposed response. Result 5 — A5 subjects B4's objection to the same interpretive authority and makes ending analysis require more coached disclosure.
- specificity: original 4 — A1's folded napkin supplies a visible, playable reaction to misinterpret. Result 5 — A1 establishes dinner, a verbal reaction and napkin folding; A5 returns to that same napkin.
- brevityWit: original 4 — A3's 'Order as emotional protection' efficiently converts a mundane correction into supposed confirmation. Result 4 — A5's 'Good boundary' briefly appears receptive before turning the refusal into another prescribed exercise.

B4 causes A5: B4 asks to eat without analysis; A5 identifies that as a boundary and prescribes its supposedly proper delivery.

Alternatives: This absorbs a direct boundary into further analysis; the biscuit alternative redirects an ordinary food request into emotional coaching.

Reservation: The language is deliberately therapeutic, but it belongs to A's fictional behavior and does not instruct the real performer. No rewrite needed.

## S99-single-b — revised

Treat every reaction as evidence you can expertly interpret; translate feelings, correct emotional misunderstandings, and prescribe the proper response.

Mechanism: A converts laughter and hunger into expert emotional diagnoses, then prescribes reassurance instead of accepting B's stated explanations.

Confusable concept: Ordinary supportive reassurance would respond to an expressed concern; A asserts hidden feelings despite B's corrections.

### before

**A:** You're laughing too quickly at that joke. Avoidance?

**B:** I thought it was funny.

**A:** Humour is often the front door to discomfort.

**B:** Could you pass the biscuits?

**A:** Seeking comfort. Which feeling would you like with them?

### after

**A:** You're laughing very quickly at Dad's impression. Nervous about meeting my parents?

**B:** No, the impression was funny.

**A:** That's approval-seeking. You can let a joke pass without earning your place.

**B:** Could you pass the biscuits? I missed lunch.

**A:** Comfort-seeking now. Say 'I belong here' before you take one. Let's feed the insecurity first.

First / second pass: 72 / 85

- cardFidelity: original 4 — A1 and A3 interpret laughter, and A5 diagnoses comfort-seeking, but no clear proper response is prescribed. Result 5 — A3 replaces B2's account of amusement with 'approval-seeking'; A5 diagnoses hunger as comfort-seeking and prescribes an affirmation.
- responseLogic: original 4 — A3 disputes B2's account of humor; A5 directly interprets B4's biscuit request. Result 4 — A3 interprets B2's enjoyment within the parent-meeting context; A5 specifically converts B4's biscuit request and missed lunch into an emotional diagnosis.
- relationship: original 3 — B4 tries moving to a practical request, but B's desired ordinary interaction stays lightly developed. Result 3 — B2 corrects the diagnosis and B4 seeks food, but B's practical needs mainly supply successive material for A's interpretation.
- heightening: original 3 — A5 applies the same labeling tactic to biscuits without increasing the consequences of A's expertise. Result 4 — A3 advises how to react to jokes; after B4 requests biscuits, A5 imposes an emotional exercise before eating.
- specificity: original 4 — A1's laugh and B4's biscuits suggest a social gathering with playable objects. Result 5 — A1 establishes meeting A's parents through Dad's impression; B4's biscuits and missed lunch ground the next exchange in an immediate need.
- brevityWit: original 3 — A5's 'Which feeling would you like with them?' reads more like a menu joke than a sincere prescription. Result 4 — A5's 'feed the insecurity first' grows directly from the biscuits and A's interpretive confidence, though it slightly explains the preceding affirmation.

B4 causes A5: B4 asks for biscuits because of a missed lunch; A5 interprets that request as comfort-seeking and attaches an affirmation to taking one.

Alternatives: The preserved dinner alternative absorbs an explicit boundary into further analysis. This alternative recasts a guest's social response and physical hunger as insecurity during a family introduction.

Reservation: A narrow pass. B has a credible immediate need, but limited influence beyond changing the diagnostic subject. The concluding metaphor is slightly over-explanatory; no rewrite is required.
