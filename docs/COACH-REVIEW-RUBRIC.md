# Imprompt coach-review rubric · coach-1.0

**Question:** Does the card explain why A says these particular things to this particular B, and does B's reply cause A's next move?

This is an editorial rubric, not an automatic humour detector. Scores express an internal reviewer's judgement. Independent blind reading and live performance must be recorded separately.

| Dimension | Weight | Evidence needed |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Card fidelity | 30 | The exact instruction, not just its category, explains A's behaviour. For pairs, the Stance changes the means of pursuing the Drive. |
| Response logic | 20 | A3 uses B2 and A5 uses B4. Facts, objects, relationship and stakes remain coherent. |
| Relationship and interaction | 15 | B has a credible want or objection and changes A's available tactics. B is not only a punchline feeder. |
| Heightening | 15 | A5 increases commitment, consequence or specificity of the established game, prompted by B4. |
| Specificity and playability | 10 | A1 establishes a situation through natural dialogue; actors can understand and play it immediately. |
| Brevity and earned wit | 10 | No explanatory padding. The comic turn follows the character's logic rather than an unrelated joke. |

Rate each 0–5: 0 absent/contradictory; 1 major failure; 2 weak; 3 workable but unpolished; 4 strong with minor reservations; 5 unusually clear and effective. Total = sum(weight × rating / 5).

## Acceptance

Internal pass: total at least 85/100; card fidelity and response logic each at least 4/5; every other dimension at least 3/5; all hard gates pass. A high wit score cannot compensate for the wrong card or a disconnected exchange. Numbers alone are insufficient: cite particular turns for each score.

Hard gates: five spoken ABABA turns; A holds the requested card(s); B's dialogue is illustrative and not an instruction to the real partner; B2 materially informs A3; B4 materially informs A5; no separate narration; no unrelated final joke; no forced fixed ending for the actual scene. If no rewrite is needed, record that decision and re-review the original without inventing improvement.

## Two-pass procedure

1. Read the exact card(s), both alternatives, and, for pairs, the actual individual-card seed examples.
2. Score the existing exchange before rewriting. Record the broken or weak link between specific turns.
3. Rewrite the whole interaction when necessary. Preserve the card meaning, not an unhelpful prop or phrasing. Explicitly document any new illustrative setting.
4. Re-read only the rewritten dialogue and exact card instructions before consulting the first-pass rationale. This is a separate internal pass, not an independent human blind test.
5. Apply the same rubric. Explain how the new B4 causes the new A5. Leave a weak revision pending; do not inflate scores to hit the threshold.
6. Compare the two alternatives: change tactic or relationship, not only a noun.
7. Save original and revised text, both ratings, evidence, source hashes, version and remaining concerns. Validate runtime output after building.

## Reference scene: S67 + D101

**Chain of Command:** Treat every question, favor, and decision as something that must travel through the proper hierarchy.

**Start the Hard Part:** Get someone else to explain, announce, or begin the emotionally difficult part.

**A:** You’re the headteacher. You should tell the parents I lost the class hamster.

**B:** You’re its teacher. You tell them.

**A:** I can supply the facts. Bad news should come from a senior position.

**B:** Fine. I’ll appoint you deputy head for the meeting.

**A:** My first recommendation is that we hear from the headteacher.

B changes A's rank to remove the excuse. A uses the new rank to send the same difficult announcement upward again. The final turn needs B's intervention: it is not a detachable punchline. The user endorsed this transcript in conversation; that endorsement does not apply to the rest of the library. The school appointment is comic logic, not advice about real school governance.
