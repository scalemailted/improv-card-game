## D119-single-a — revised

Agree to be honest, hopeful, or vulnerable only in forms that preserve your emotional safety.

Mechanism: D119 makes A agree to emotional openness only within conditions that preserve A's sense of safety.

Confusable concept: Avoidance refuses engagement; A offers a limited, feasible form of engagement and specifies the protection it requires.

### before

**A:** I'll talk about the argument, but not the whole year tonight.

**B:** The whole year matters.

**A:** It does. Tonight I can finish one sentence without leaving.

**B:** Where do we start?

**A:** With the argument, and permission to pause.

### after

**A:** I'll talk about last night's rent argument, but not the whole year tonight.

**B:** The whole year matters. You've been pulling away.

**A:** It does. One question, and let me finish before you answer.

**B:** All right. Were you going to move out?

**A:** I packed a bag. It's still upstairs. Please let that be the whole answer for a minute.

Result 5 — B2's year-long concern causes A3 to permit a question under safer conditions; B4's moving-out question elicits A5's concrete admission.

Reservation: This is stronger as a vulnerable dramatic exchange than as comedy. Its exact behavioral progression is clear enough to pass without a rewrite.

## D119-single-b — revised

Agree to be honest, hopeful, or vulnerable only in forms that preserve your emotional safety.

Mechanism: D119 makes A's willingness to be vulnerable depend on an emotionally safe form of participation.

Confusable concept: Planning an escape aims at departure; A uses the available exit to make continued engagement possible.

### before

**A:** I'll sit by the door while we talk.

**B:** Are you planning to run?

**A:** Knowing I can leave is helping me stay.

**B:** We can stop when you need to.

**A:** Good. Now I can risk a less careful answer.

### after

**A:** I'll sit by the door while we talk about trying again.

**B:** Are you planning to run?

**A:** Knowing I can leave is helping me stay.

**B:** We can stop when you need to.

**A:** I want to come home. Please don't hand me a key yet; saying it is enough for tonight.

First / second pass: 90 / 88

- cardFidelity: original 5 — A3 explicitly makes the ability to leave the condition for staying, and A5 risks greater honesty after B4 supplies safety. Result 5 — A1 requires a door-side seat, A3 explains escape as the condition for staying, and A5 separates admitting a wish from accepting a key.
- responseLogic: original 5 — A3 corrects B2's interpretation of the door seat; A5 directly uses B4's permission to stop. Result 5 — A3 directly answers B2's fear that A will run; B4's permission to stop enables A5's wish to come home with a new limit.
- relationship: original 4 — B2 fears departure and B4 responds with an accommodation that changes A's willingness to speak. Result 4 — B2 reveals anxiety about A leaving, while B4 offers control over stopping so the reconciliation conversation can continue.
- heightening: original 4 — A5 advances from arranging a safe seat to risking a less guarded answer because of B4. Result 4 — After B4 assures safety, A5 risks naming the desired reconciliation but protects against immediately making it concrete through a key.
- specificity: original 4 — A1's door-side seat provides a concrete safety condition, though the conversation's topic remains open. Result 4 — A1 establishes a reconciliation conversation and chosen seat; A5's home and key make the stakes tangible, though their history remains broad.
- brevityWit: original 4 — A3's 'Knowing I can leave is helping me stay' expresses the character's paradox directly and economically. Result 3 — A3's 'Knowing I can leave is helping me stay' is economical; A5 earns its boundary but supplies limited comic surprise.

B4 causes A5: B4 grants permission to stop; A5 consequently volunteers a vulnerable wish while withholding permission for immediate practical commitment.

Alternatives: Here B's reassurance opens voluntary vulnerability; the rent-argument alternative uses a negotiated question to elicit an admission about an existing plan to leave.

Reservation: The key is introduced only in A5, although coming home makes it intelligible. The tone is primarily tender rather than witty. No rewrite needed.
