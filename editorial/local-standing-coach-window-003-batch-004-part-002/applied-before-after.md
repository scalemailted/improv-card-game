## S167-single-a — revised

Choose one arbitrary detail and treat it as the key that explains, controls, or prevents the entire impossible situation.

Mechanism: A assigns an arbitrary small detail decisive control over an entire impossible situation and organizes practical choices around preserving it.

Confusable concept: General magical superstition can accumulate charms; this card commits to one chosen detail as the controlling key.

### before

**A:** Keep that pebble level. It's the only thing holding Tuesday here.

**B:** It's an ordinary pebble.

**A:** Exactly why Tuesday trusts it.

**B:** Can I put it down?

**A:** On a level surface. Wednesday's waiting for a mistake.

### after

**A:** Our ferry's floating above the harbour. Keep that souvenir pebble level; it's the only thing stopping us tipping.

**B:** I picked it up for you. I need my hands for the bags.

**A:** Put it in my lunchbox. I'll keep the ferry level.

**B:** Your sandwiches make it tilt.

**A:** I'll flatten them into a level bed. Lunch can suffer; the passengers mustn't slide off.

First / second pass: 90 / 87

- cardFidelity: original 5 — A1 makes a pebble's level position responsible for holding Tuesday; A3 uses its ordinariness as supporting evidence. Result 5 — A1 makes an arbitrary souvenir pebble the sole safeguard against the airborne ferry tipping. A3 and A5 consistently organize action around keeping that detail level.
- responseLogic: original 5 — A3 directly absorbs B2's ordinary-pebble objection; B4's request to put it down determines A5's level-surface condition. Result 5 — B2 needs free hands for bags, so A3 proposes transferring the pebble to A's lunchbox. B4 identifies the sandwiches as the source of tilt, so A5 proposes flattening them.
- relationship: original 4 — B4 wants relief from holding the pebble, and A5 offers a constrained way to achieve it. Result 4 — B2 reveals that the pebble was a gift for A and asserts a practical luggage need. B4 tests A3's proposed solution, making B a participant with useful objections.
- heightening: original 4 — A5 makes the proposed handover carry the risk of Wednesday exploiting a mistake. Result 3 — A5 sacrifices lunch and specifies the danger to passengers after B4's sandwich objection. This develops the game, but the ferry-wide stakes were already established in A1.
- specificity: original 4 — A1 gives B a small object and an immediate holding requirement, though the surrounding location remains open. Result 4 — A1 establishes a floating ferry and a level pebble; B2 and A3 supply bags and a lunchbox. The opening states the fantastic premise somewhat directly, but the physical task is clear.
- brevityWit: original 4 — A3's explanation of Tuesday's trust economically reinforces the arbitrary detail instead of introducing another object. Result 4 — A3's lunchbox solution is concise and usable. A5's willingness to flatten lunch earns its comic disproportion through B4, although the passenger explanation slightly underlines the joke.

B4 causes A5: B4 reveals that the lunchbox contents undermine level support. A5 changes those contents by proposing to flatten the sandwiches.

Alternatives: The preserved kettle alternative protects a scratch while negotiating B's desire for tea. This candidate accepts responsibility for a gift and adapts a cooperative solution to B's luggage needs; its tactic is accommodating rather than initially prohibitive.

Reservation: Heightening is modest: A5 mainly repairs the previous solution. The concrete sacrifice and uninterrupted causal chain support acceptance without a rewrite.

## S167-single-b — revised

Choose one arbitrary detail and treat it as the key that explains, controls, or prevents the entire impossible situation.

Mechanism: A treats one arbitrary physical detail as the key to sustaining an impossible cosmic condition.

Confusable concept: Operating an established magical device follows known controls; this card elevates an incidental detail into the decisive cause.

### before

**A:** Don't press the third button on the kettle. That's the moon's dimmer.

**B:** How do we make tea?

**A:** Use the first two. Tea and lunar stability are separate jobs.

**B:** The third one's flashing.

**A:** Then the moon wants attention. Don't reward it.

### after

**A:** Don't press the kettle's third button. That tiny scratch on it is keeping the moon lit.

**B:** That's the boil button. I want tea.

**A:** Use a saucepan. We can't risk smoothing the scratch.

**B:** I'll press it with my fingernail, around the scratch.

**A:** Let me guide your hand. Everyone reading by moonlight is relying on this cup.

First / second pass: 77 / 90

- cardFidelity: original 4 — A1 selects the third kettle button as the moon's dimmer, though this can read as an actual magical control rather than A's arbitrary attribution. Result 5 — A1 singles out a tiny scratch as sustaining moonlight; A3 protects it and A5 supervises its preservation during use.
- responseLogic: original 4 — A3 offers B2 a tea method; A5 interprets B4's flashing button, but changes from dimming to attention-seeking. Result 5 — B2's tea need prompts A3's saucepan alternative; B4's fingernail workaround prompts A5's guided-hand compromise.
- relationship: original 4 — B2 wants tea, and B4 reports a device change that complicates A's proposed separation of functions. Result 4 — B2 wants ordinary tea and B4 bargains with a method that accommodates A's scratch theory.
- heightening: original 3 — A5 advises ignoring the flash without increasing the cost or commitment of protecting lunar stability. Result 4 — A5 accepts B4's risky method while taking personal control and extending responsibility to everyone reading by moonlight.
- specificity: original 4 — A1 establishes a kettle with numbered buttons and an immediate prohibition. Result 4 — A1 locates the scratch on a kettle button; B2 and B4 give actors concrete tea-making and fingernail actions to discuss.
- brevityWit: original 4 — A3's separate jobs for tea and lunar stability is concise, while A5 introduces a looser parenting joke. Result 4 — A5's reliance of moonlight readers on 'this cup' earns its scale contrast from the negotiated button press.

B4 causes A5: B4 proposes pressing around the scratch; A5 agrees to guide the hand through that precise operation.

Alternatives: Alternative b uses bargaining and a shared precision task; a grants conditional release from holding a pebble.

Reservation: No rewrite needed. A1's kettle with a third button is slightly unusual but immediately understandable.
