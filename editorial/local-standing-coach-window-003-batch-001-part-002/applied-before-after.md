## S131-single-a — revised

Treat one person as the only safe place for your honest reactions, while hiding how much their discretion determines what you can risk.

Mechanism: A reserves honest reactions for one trusted person while disguising how that person's discretion controls the risks A takes.

Confusable concept: Ordinary confidentiality asks openly for secrecy without concealing emotional or practical dependence on the listener.

### before

**A:** Close the office door. You're the only person getting this version.

**B:** You could tell the team.

**A:** They can have the weather. This needs your discretion.

**B:** What's wrong?

**A:** Let me hear the latch first, then I'll stop editing.

### after

**A:** Close the office door. I hated the director's presentation. You're the only person I can say that to.

**B:** He asked me to collect feedback.

**A:** Put me down as engaged. This is just us having coffee.

**B:** I'll leave your comments out.

**A:** Thanks. The standing ovation felt like a hostage video. More coffee?

First / second pass: 48 / 92

- cardFidelity: original 2 — A3 explicitly demands discretion; A5 openly makes honesty conditional on the latch, exposing the dependence A should hide. Result 5 — A1 singles out B as the sole confidant; A3 disguises exposure as coffee, and A5 casually resumes honesty after reassurance.
- responseLogic: original 3 — A3 answers B2's team suggestion, but A5 returns to A1's door without materially using B4's question. Result 5 — B2's feedback role causes A3's public/private distinction; B4's promise permits A5's sharper criticism.
- relationship: original 2 — B2 suggests another audience; B4 merely asks what is wrong and supplies no personal stake. Result 4 — B2 has a real reporting responsibility; B4 chooses to protect A, though that choice comes readily.
- heightening: original 2 — A5 repeats A1's request for privacy without increasing the risk or revealing an honest reaction. Result 4 — After B4 promises omission, A5 risks the more pointed 'hostage video' comparison.
- specificity: original 3 — A1 establishes an office and team, but A3 and A5 never identify what A needs to discuss. Result 5 — A1's office door and director's presentation establish an immediately playable confidential workplace exchange.
- brevityWit: original 3 — A3's weather contrast is economical, while A5's 'stop editing' keeps the scene abstract. Result 4 — A3's 'engaged' euphemism and A5's coffee offer economically conceal the importance of B's discretion.

B4 causes A5: B4 removes the reporting risk, enabling A5 to disclose a harsher honest reaction.

Alternatives: The office alternative manages official reporting; the notebook alternative hides family disloyalty through self-deprecation.

Reservation: B4 grants protection without much competing pressure. No rewrite needed.

## S131-single-b — revised

Treat one person as the only safe place for your honest reactions, while hiding how much their discretion determines what you can risk.

Mechanism: A limits honest reactions to one confidant and minimizes the significance of the protection that makes those reactions possible.

Confusable concept: Fear of disclosure alone would explain hiding the notebook but would not require disguising dependence on this particular person.

### before

**A:** I wrote the truth in this notebook. Only you can read it.

**B:** You don't need to trust just me.

**A:** Naturally. I'm simply starting with the entire current list.

**B:** Will you tell anyone else?

**A:** After I know what honesty sounds like in a safe room.

### after

**A:** You're the only one who's read my notebook. I don't want to take over Dad's shop.

**B:** He wants me to bring it to Sunday lunch.

**A:** Bring the accounts notebook. This one's mostly spelling mistakes.

**B:** I'll keep this one here.

**A:** Could I add something? I was relieved when the freezer broke. Terrible spelling on that page.

First / second pass: 56 / 93

- cardFidelity: original 3 — A1 restricts the notebook to B, but A5 directly announces the need for a safe room instead of hiding its importance. Result 5 — A1 makes B the notebook's exclusive reader; A3 and A5 camouflage consequential admissions as spelling mistakes.
- responseLogic: original 4 — A3 answers B2's wider-trust suggestion, and A5 gives a condition in response to B4's question about others. Result 5 — B2's lunch request causes A3's notebook substitution; B4's safekeeping enables A5's additional confession.
- relationship: original 2 — B2 and B4 both ask A to consider other confidants without establishing B's own interest. Result 4 — B2 carries Dad's request and B4 protects A against it, establishing divided family obligations.
- heightening: original 2 — A5 restates the exclusive safety already established by A1 rather than developing a concrete risk. Result 5 — A5 moves beyond rejecting succession to admitting relief at a setback to Dad's shop after B4 secures the notebook.
- specificity: original 2 — A1 supplies a notebook, but A3 and A5 leave its truth and the relationship unidentified. Result 4 — A1 establishes Dad's shop and a private notebook; B2's unspecified 'it' leaves Dad's knowledge slightly uncertain.
- brevityWit: original 3 — A3's 'entire current list' is compact wit; A5's abstract formulation sounds composed rather than immediate. Result 4 — A3's spelling excuse returns in A5 as a compact cover for the more troubling freezer admission.

B4 causes A5: B4 promises to retain the private notebook, giving A5 sufficient safety to add a worse admission.

Alternatives: This uses a private written record and family expectations; alternative a uses conversational discretion inside workplace reporting.

Reservation: Why Dad requested this particular notebook is lightly sketched. No rewrite needed.
