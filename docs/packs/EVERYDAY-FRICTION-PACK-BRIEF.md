# Imprompt Pack Brief — Everyday Friction

**Pack ID:** `everyday-friction`  
**Reserved IDs:** S25–S48 and D25–D48  
**Target release:** Imprompt v0.9.0  
**In-app status:** Playtest  
**Candidate pool:** 64 cards drafted; 48 selected; 8 held; 8 rejected

## Creative promise

**Everyday Friction finds major character behavior inside minor practical problems.** The pack turns chores, schedules, shared spaces, favors, handoffs, etiquette, maintenance, small expenses, forgotten details, and routine inconveniences into scene pressure without prescribing a location, occupation, relationship, or joke.

A card belongs when an ordinary task or inconvenience gives the holder a strong way to behave. The stakes may remain objectively small while becoming emotionally, socially, or philosophically important to the character.

## What makes this pack distinct

Core Foundations establishes broad dramatic engines. Everyday Friction narrows those engines into recognizable daily behavior:

- authority expressed through tiny decisions and procedures;
- history revealed through habits, reminders, and accumulated favors;
- emotion expressed through usefulness, preparation, inconvenience, and invisible labor;
- absurdity built from routines, contingency plans, practical details, and escalating minor problems;
- objectives pursued through chores, schedules, responsibility, shared resources, and etiquette;
- secrets hidden inside misplaced objects, unfinished work, changed plans, and quiet repairs;
- recurring behavior built from scorekeeping, optimization, accommodation, bargaining, and technical compliance.

The cards remain portable. “Everyday” describes the kind of pressure, not a required realistic setting. These prompts should work between roommates, coworkers, relatives, adventurers, monarchs, ghosts, astronauts, or circus performers.

## Desired player experience

A player should recognize the human behavior almost immediately:

- “I know someone who does this.”
- “I have done this.”
- “I can show this without explaining it.”
- “I can repeat and heighten this at least three times.”

The comedy should come from committed behavior and collisions between private rules—not from a prewritten punchline.

## Included territory

- shared responsibilities and uneven effort;
- lateness, schedules, waiting, and changing plans;
- borrowed, misplaced, damaged, or overprotected objects;
- chores, maintenance, cleanup, preparation, and follow-through;
- favors that accumulate into obligation;
- routine expertise and tiny standards;
- hosting, accommodation, courtesy, and suppressed inconvenience;
- shortcuts, substitutes, questionable fixes, and improvised completion;
- messages, reminders, handoffs, and unclear responsibility;
- shared spaces, supplies, access, and practical control;
- small choices treated as evidence about the relationship;
- ordinary systems treated as sacred, doomed, or secretly meaningful.

## Excluded territory

This pack should not rely on:

- a fixed occupation, household, workplace, store, restaurant, or service encounter;
- a specific prop such as dishes, keys, a car, or a phone unless the instruction generalizes beyond it;
- institutional bureaucracy as the central engine; reserve that for Rules, Rituals & Institutions;
- overt hierarchy or coercive power as the primary focus; reserve that for Power Games;
- deep romantic, familial, or traumatic history as the primary focus; reserve that for Relationship Knots or Emotional Pressure;
- complex deception plots or criminal schemes; reserve that for Secrets & Schemes;
- impossible premises as the only source of comedy; reserve that for Absurd Commitment;
- humiliation, stereotypes, unwanted contact, or material that requires personal disclosure.

## Voice

- Contemporary plain English.
- Familiar behavior described precisely.
- Titles should sound like phrases people might recognize in ordinary conflict.
- Instructions should be immediately playable and usually 8–22 words.
- One central engine per card.
- No assumption that the other performer knows or follows the rule.
- No declaration of another performer’s private thoughts, feelings, or future behavior.

## Metadata additions

This pack introduces controlled motifs for ordinary-scene analysis:

`communication`, `convenience`, `etiquette`, `fairness`, `maintenance`, `mess`, `resources`, `responsibility`, `routine`, `space`, `time`, and `work`.

They remain internal metadata; the visible category system is unchanged.

## Candidate-production result

The editorial pool contains 64 candidates:

- 32 Stance candidates;
- 32 Drive candidates;
- 48 selected for the required subtheme matrix;
- 8 held for possible revision or reassignment to later packs;
- 8 rejected because of duplication, category ambiguity, or a weaker playable engine.

Selection favored observable behavior, repeatability, portability, difference from Core Foundations, and concise phone-readable copy.

## Playtest questions

1. Can a player understand the card in five seconds?
2. Does the card produce visible behavior before the player explains a premise?
3. Can the player repeat or heighten it three times?
4. Does it remain playable when the scene partner reacts unexpectedly?
5. Does it feel meaningfully different from its Core Foundations card in the same subtheme?
6. Does the card work outside a literal domestic or workplace scene?
7. Is the card frequently vetoed, abandoned, or misread?
8. Do any two cards repeatedly generate the same scene pattern?
9. Are any titles or instructions awkward on a small phone?
10. Should a held alternate replace a selected card after live testing?
