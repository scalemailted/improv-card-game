## S152-single-a — revised

Carry one past favor as sufficient reason to expect an impossible act in return, and present the exchange as perfectly fair.

Mechanism: A values a concrete past favor as sufficient payment for an impossible return and calmly treats that accounting as fair.

Confusable concept: Enforcing an impossible promise would make B's wording the source of obligation instead of A's earlier kindness.

### before

**A:** You owe me lunch money. Bring back ten minutes of yesterday.

**B:** I meant I'd buy lunch.

**A:** You said anything. I kept the word.

**B:** I can't travel through time.

**A:** Then you'll need to start earlier.

### after

**A:** I covered your lunch yesterday. Bring back the ten minutes I spent queuing.

**B:** I'll buy your lunch today.

**A:** That covers the sandwich. I'm only asking for my time back.

**B:** I can queue for you today.

**A:** Thanks. Use that time to retrieve yesterday's minutes. Then we're square.

- relationship: original 3 — B2 offers lunch and B4 states a limit, providing a credible debtor's resistance. Result 4 — B2 and B4 offer increasingly relevant compensation, giving A actual solutions to reinterpret.
- heightening: original 2 — A5 supplies a time pun without increasing the repayment obligation or its practical consequences. Result 3 — A5 recruits today's queueing into the demand, but mostly restates A1's unchanged ten-minute debt.
- specificity: original 3 — A1 names lunch money but gives no reason A wants those particular ten minutes of yesterday. Result 4 — A1's lunch queue and ten minutes make the impossible claim concrete, though the present setting remains light.
- brevityWit: original 3 — A3 is concise, while A5's detachable 'start earlier' joke weakens the exchange's specific logic. Result 4 — A3's sandwich/time distinction is economical; A5's 'Then we're square' sustains the sincere fairness claim.

Reservation: No rewrite needed for a narrow internal pass. A5 changes the proposed means more than the scale or consequence.

## S152-single-b — revised

Carry one past favor as sufficient reason to expect an impossible act in return, and present the exchange as perfectly fair.

Mechanism: A treats moving a sofa as fair consideration for moving the moon and uses the favor's difficulty to defend the exchange.

Confusable concept: General entitlement could demand a miracle without identifying a prior favor or arguing reciprocal fairness.

### before

**A:** I helped move your sofa. Move the moon away from my window.

**B:** Those aren't equivalent favours.

**A:** The sofa had stairs. The moon's already airborne.

**B:** How would I do it?

**A:** I didn't ask how heavy the sofa was before helping.

### after

**A:** I helped move your sofa. Move the moon away from my window.

**B:** Those aren't equivalent favours.

**A:** The sofa had stairs. The moon's already airborne.

**B:** I'll move your bed away from the window. I have twenty minutes.

**A:** Then move the moon for twenty minutes each night. I didn't leave your sofa halfway upstairs.

- cardFidelity: original 5 — A1 exchanges sofa-moving for moon-moving; A3 sincerely argues that stairs make the past favor harder. Result 5 — A1 makes moving a sofa grounds for moving the moon; A3 sincerely balances stairs against airborne convenience, presenting the impossible repayment as fair.
- responseLogic: original 5 — A3 answers B2's equivalence objection; A5 uses B4's procedural question to demand reciprocal unquestioning help. Result 5 — B2 disputes equivalence, prompting A3's comparison of physical effort. B4 offers twenty minutes, which A5 converts into a nightly lunar obligation.
- relationship: original 4 — B2 disputes the debt's scale and B4 seeks a method, allowing A to switch from comparison to obligation. Result 4 — B2 challenges the debt calculation; B4 offers practical help with the bed and a time limit, giving B a credible helpful boundary.
- heightening: original 4 — A5 raises the moral demand from equivalent labor to helping without prior investigation. Result 4 — A5 turns B4's finite availability into recurring service and invokes finishing the sofa move to justify completing the impossible repayment.
- specificity: original 5 — A1 supplies sofa, moon and window; A3 adds stairs as the precise basis for A's accounting. Result 4 — A1 supplies the past sofa move and present window problem; B4's bed-moving offer makes the domestic situation immediately playable.
- brevityWit: original 5 — A3's 'already airborne' earns its joke through the moving comparison, and A5 sustains that same logic. Result 4 — A3's 'already airborne' compresses A's unfair accounting neatly; A5's 'halfway upstairs' earns its return through the original favor.

B4 causes A5: B4 offers bed relocation within twenty minutes. A5 adopts that duration while redirecting the help to nightly moon relocation.

Alternatives: The preserved lunch alternative distinguishes sandwich repayment from lost time and absorbs a helpful queueing offer. This alternative argues comparative effort and converts a friend's limited availability into recurring labor.

Reservation: A5's nightly schedule is a slightly convenient expansion of B4's one-time availability, but its opportunistic accounting remains clear. No rewrite needed.
