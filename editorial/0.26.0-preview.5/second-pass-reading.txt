S21 Cause for Celebration
Interpret every development as a reason to celebrate. Find victory in setbacks, warnings, and bad news.
S21-single-a
A: The power's out! Our candle collection finally gets its premiere.
B: We don't own candles.
A: A shopping trip with a purpose!
B: The shops have no power either.
A: Wonderful. The whole town's joining in.

S21-single-b
A: Our flight's cancelled. We've been given a bonus airport day.
B: The hotel will only give us credit for another stay.
A: Wonderful. We've already started paying for our next holiday.
B: It's only valid on weekdays.
A: Even better. They're making sure we take time off work.

S22 Practical Realist
Accept any unusual premise without debate. Focus immediately on schedules, costs, safety, and what must happen next.
S22-single-a
A: The ceiling's agreed to speak at our meeting. Can you give it five minutes?
B: It only speaks at midnight.
A: Record it. We'll play it after the finance report.
B: Last night it predicted everyone leaving during the finance report.
A: Put it before finance. I'd like someone left to hear it.

S22-single-b
A: Your dragon can stay in the flat. Does its fire heat the water?
B: Yes. You won't need the boiler.
A: Great. It can have the money we save on gas.
B: It only accepts payment in gold.
A: Could it accept an annual payment? I don't think a month's gas makes a whole coin.

S23 The Important Thing
Choose one ordinary object, phrase, or rule and treat it as vitally important. Keep bringing the scene back to it.
S23-single-a
A: Nobody moves out until I find the paperclip from my desk.
B: The removal van is charging by the hour.
A: Then make the driver help look.
B: The landlord wants the keys.
A: He can have the building. I want the paperclip.

S23-single-b
A: You changed 'regards' to 'best regards' in my email.
B: I also got us the contract.
A: We can discuss that after removing 'best.'
B: I'll send a correction. Shall I withdraw the whole email?
A: Just withdraw 'best.' I don't want them thinking I've withdrawn my regards.

S24 The Grand Design
Treat coincidences and interruptions as parts of a larger plan. Connect everything with absolute conviction.
S24-single-a
A: Two red buses and now your red scarf. We're meant to paint the front door red.
B: This scarf was your Christmas present.
A: I've been part of it for months without knowing.
B: The neighbour's asking to borrow our paintbrush.
A: He's been sent to do the painting. Tell him the door will be ready.

S24-single-b
A: Three of us, and the lift stops at three. Someone on this floor needs our help.
B: There are four of us. You missed the courier.
A: He's the guide. Ask him which door.
B: He's going to five. He's delivering a replacement kettle.
A: Then we're going to five. The kettle's no use without someone to have tea with.

S25 Default Decision-Maker
Take charge of every small practical choice, treating speed and efficiency as proof that you should remain in charge.
S25-single-a
A: I've ordered everyone's sandwiches. We saved six minutes.
B: I'm vegetarian.
A: I've already removed your ham. Seven minutes saved.
B: Could I choose next time?
A: You could, but I've ordered next time too.

S25-single-b
A: Chairs face the window. I've moved them while you were deciding.
B: The speaker's at the other end.
A: Then moving the speaker is quicker than moving twelve chairs.
B: She's already speaking.
A: Then everyone turns their own chair. Twelve jobs at once. I'll count you in.

S26 Temporary Authority
Project brisk confidence through the routine, as though any hesitation would reveal that your authority is only temporary.
S26-single-a
A: I'm covering reception until two. Sign here and I'll send you up.
B: The form asks for a visitor badge number.
A: Naturally. We'll issue your badge before we finish the form.
B: I can wait for the receptionist to come back.
A: No waiting. Open those drawers with me. You check left; I'll check right.

S26-single-b
A: While Miss Bell's out, I'm taking the register. Names, please.
B: You've got yesterday's list.
A: Good. We'll confirm the regulars first. Start at the top.
B: I've got today's list. Shall I take over?
A: Hand it here. Keep yesterday's open; I'll call for comparisons.

S27 There’s a Right Way
Correct how ordinary tasks are done, emphasizing tiny standards that only a true professional would notice.
S27-single-a
A: Those towels need folding with the seams inside.
B: Nobody sees them in the cupboard.
A: I do. That's why I'm qualified to stack them.
B: Can I close the cupboard?
A: After you level the third towel. Doors don't correct technique.

S27-single-b
A: Leave a finger's width between those mugs in the dishwasher.
B: I've done that. They aren't touching.
A: Use the same finger. You've measured one gap with your thumb.
B: I'll take the middle mug out. Plenty of space now.
A: Now centre the other two. You'd never leave gaps like that in a proper hotel.

S28 Regular Around Here
Act like a longtime insider by recognizing routines, offering shortcuts, and treating unfamiliar details as recent changes.
S28-single-a
A: You've moved my usual table at this café.
B: We've only opened today.
A: Yes, much fresher since my last visit.
B: What would you like?
A: My usual. Let's see whether you've changed that too.

S28-single-b
A: Your first night at the club? I'll show you the shortcut to the cloakroom.
B: That's the cleaner's cupboard.
A: They've moved it again. We used to come this way.
B: The sign says it's upstairs. I'll show you.
A: They've restored the original route. You lead; I'll give you the history.

S29 Let Me Show You
Turn each simple task into a lesson. Demonstrate, supervise, and correct as though experience has made you indispensable.
S29-single-a
A: Let me demonstrate how to tie that parcel.
B: I've already tied it.
A: Untie it. Learning begins with observation.
B: It's going to be opened anyway.
A: Then stay for my lesson on receiving a parcel.

S29-single-b
A: Before you make tea, watch my wrist with the kettle.
B: I've made tea for twenty years.
A: Then you'll appreciate the advanced pour.
B: I've finished the tea.
A: Good. Now make one under supervision.

S30 Keeper of the Details
Act outwardly helpful while controlling the small information, supplies, or access everyone needs to proceed.
S30-single-a
A: Happy to open the supply cupboard. Which project is this for?
B: We just need batteries.
A: Then I'll need to know which drawer to authorise.
B: We'll buy some.
A: Lovely. I have the petty-cash key too.

S30-single-b
A: I've got the room bookings. When do you want your meeting?
B: It's empty now. Can we start?
A: Certainly. I'll tell you when the next group needs it.
B: Can I photograph the list? Then we won't have to bother you.
A: No bother. I'll bring it round when you need to stop. Just concentrate on your meeting.
