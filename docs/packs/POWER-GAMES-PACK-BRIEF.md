# Imprompt Pack Brief — Power Games

**Pack ID:** `power-games`  
**Reserved IDs:** S49–S72 and D49–D72  
**Target release:** Imprompt v0.10.0  
**In-app status:** Playtest  
**Candidate pool:** 64 cards drafted; 48 selected; 8 held; 8 rejected

## Creative promise

**Power Games turns the struggle over who gets to decide into visible, repeatable character behavior.** The pack explores authority, legitimacy, leverage, dependency, hierarchy, access, patronage, succession, compliance, and resistance without requiring a political office, workplace title, military rank, or literal throne.

A card belongs when the holder can reveal a relationship to power through choices: issuing rulings, protecting a fragile mandate, controlling access, invoking past support, demanding public respect, locating the real decision-maker, trading privileges, testing obedience, concealing dependence, or expanding jurisdiction.

## Central comic question

> **Who gets to decide, and what makes that authority real?**

The answer may come from rank, expertise, history, confidence, resources, information, ritual, reputation, access, cooperation, or a symbol everyone has agreed to respect. The comedy comes from performers committing to different private theories of power and discovering how those theories collide.

## What makes this pack distinct

Core Foundations contains broad status and objective engines. Everyday Friction finds character behavior inside small practical strain. Power Games focuses specifically on the **mechanics and legitimacy of influence**:

- authority may be formal, borrowed, inherited, performed, conditional, or quietly exercised;
- history may function as patronage, debt, seniority, rivalry, or an old challenge to legitimacy;
- emotion may attach belonging and self-worth to visible respect or proximity to influence;
- worldview cards may interpret every interaction as hierarchy, jurisdiction, ritual, or a contest over symbols;
- objectives seek endorsement, obedience, allegiance, surrender, coalition, access, or recognition;
- secrets protect the source, weakness, dependency, or consequences of authority;
- repeatable behaviors rank, bargain, reframe, absorb consensus, and expand control.

## Included territory

- formal and informal authority;
- legitimacy, credentials, titles, rank, and rightful belonging;
- hidden leverage and control of access, resources, information, or approval;
- patronage, sponsorship, coalition-building, and conditional support;
- succession, replacement, mentorship, seniority, and rivalry for leadership;
- dependency disguised as command or independence;
- obedience, dissent, compliance, and challenges made through proxies;
- public respect, symbolic privileges, and ceremonial status;
- authority that exists only because others cooperate;
- the gap between official titles and practical power;
- reframing setbacks as strategy and responsibility as influence;
- expanding jurisdiction whenever a new problem appears.

## Excluded territory

This pack should not rely on:

- a required office, government, military, monarchy, school, or corporate setting;
- threats of physical harm, forced contact, humiliation, or demeaning identity-based status;
- a command that can only work if the other performer obeys exactly;
- a fixed claim about what another performer secretly thinks, wants, or feels;
- ordinary task disputes without a meaningful question of influence or legitimacy; those belong in Everyday Friction;
- procedural bureaucracy as the primary engine; reserve that for Rules, Rituals & Institutions;
- elaborate conspiracy or crime plots; reserve those for Secrets & Schemes;
- winning as the sole engine; reserve pure contest structures for Competition & Consequences;
- a prewritten tyrant joke, throne joke, or boss stereotype.

## Portability rule

“Power” describes the behavior, not the setting. Every selected card should work among friends, relatives, neighbors, performers, adventurers, service workers, ghosts, astronauts, circus employees, or two people deciding where to sit.

Too narrow:

> Order your employee to finish the quarterly report.

Portable:

> **Delegate Downward** — Make someone accept an unwanted responsibility by placing them lower in the hierarchy.

## Hidden-information rule

Each instruction may govern only its holder. A Stance can define how the holder reads status, behaves around authority, or protects legitimacy. It cannot declare that the other performer admires, fears, obeys, or challenges them.

Weak:

> They are intimidated by your rank.

Strong:

> **Public Respect** — Seek visible signs of respect and treat private appreciation as insufficient.

A Drive may pursue a response, but it must leave the other performer free to resist, reinterpret, or counter the attempt.

## Desired player experience

A player should be able to identify:

1. the first visible power move;
2. what can repeat or escalate;
3. what would threaten the character’s position;
4. how the card remains playable when the partner refuses the implied hierarchy;
5. why this card differs from a generic high-status performance.

Strong scenes should reveal that power is relational. A character may command, but their authority still depends on someone accepting the title, needing the resource, respecting the ritual, or remaining in the room.

## Difficulty and tone profile

Power Games is intentionally more demanding than Everyday Friction. It should remain accessible to beginners while offering enough contradiction and indirect leverage for veteran performers.

## Metadata additions

This pack adds reusable coach roles:

`broker`, `challenger`, `claimant`, `enforcer`, `gatekeeper`, `patron`, and `successor`.

It also adds controlled motifs:

`access`, `allegiance`, `autonomy`, `compliance`, `hierarchy`, `influence`, `leverage`, `legitimacy`, `patronage`, `reputation`, `succession`, and `territory`.

These remain internal metadata. The visible seven-category system does not change.

## Exercise value

The pack materially deepens existing coach presets:

- **Status Clash** gains six new authority Stances and twelve new Direct Objectives;
- **Crown & Heart** gains stronger legitimacy, approval, dependence, and control collisions;
- **Past & Future** gains patronage, succession, old-rank, and unresolved-authority material;
- custom exercises can use richer pools for gatekeeper, challenger, broker, claimant, or successor dynamics.

Every phone still shuffles independently. A shared exercise configuration never shares cards, draw order, or Scene Log history.

## Playtest questions

1. Does the card create visible behavior rather than generic “high status” posing?
2. Can the player understand the instruction in five seconds?
3. Does the power tactic remain playable when the scene partner resists it?
4. Can the behavior repeat or heighten at least three times?
5. Does the card leave room for multiple relationships and settings?
6. Does it feel distinct from Core Foundations and Everyday Friction cards in the same subtheme?
7. Do Mirror exercises create productive collision rather than mutual stonewalling?
8. Do Paired exercises create complementary pressure rather than predetermine a winner?
9. Are any cards frequently vetoed because they feel coercive, vague, or too abstract?
10. Should a held alternate replace a selected card after live testing?
