Mechanism: A maintains the full emotional burden of an unnamed rupture while each new detail changes whether it means absence, chosen distance or listening without joining.

Confusable concept: A fixed grievance would use every detail to repeat one accusation; A changes the understood nature of the injury without resolving it.

### before

**A:** I still haven't forgiven you for missing my concert.

**B:** I was outside. They wouldn't let me in.

**A:** Then you were close enough to hear and still didn't call?

**B:** I heard you dedicate a song to me.

**A:** So you knew exactly how abandoned I felt, in perfect acoustics.

### after

**A:** I can give you another concert ticket. That doesn't mean we're past that evening.

**B:** I was outside the door the whole time.

**A:** So it wasn't distance. You were that close and left me alone with it.

**B:** I heard you say my name. I thought you wanted me to stay out.

**A:** Then what I carried as silence was you listening. I don't know how to hand you this ticket now.

First / second pass: 70 / 90

- cardFidelity: original 2 — A1 explicitly names missing the concert as the rupture, contradicting the unnamed requirement despite A3 and A5 preserving its weight. Result 5 — A1 leaves the rupture unnamed; A3 changes absence into nearby abandonment, and A5 changes silence into listening without easing the hurt.
- responseLogic: original 4 — A3 uses B2's proximity and A5 uses B4's heard dedication, though perfect acoustics overstates what B established. Result 5 — B2's position outside the door drives A3's proximity accusation; B4's hearing A's name drives A5's reinterpretation of silence.
- relationship: original 4 — B2 offers exclusion as an exculpatory detail and B4 reveals having heard a personally meaningful dedication. Result 4 — B2 and B4 disclose proximity and a misunderstood cue, giving B a perspective beyond simple denial.
- heightening: original 4 — B4's evidence of attention becomes A5's stronger charge that B understood the abandonment. Result 4 — A5 makes the new understanding interfere with the ticket A1 was prepared to offer, increasing the present consequence.
- specificity: original 5 — A1's concert, B2's barred entrance and B4's dedication create a coherent shared event. Result 4 — A1's concert ticket and B2's door ground the unnamed incident, though the reason B4 heard exclusion remains deliberately uncertain.
- brevityWit: original 4 — A5's acoustics phrase draws on B4's hearing, but adds polish at the expense of emotional plainness. Result 4 — A3's 'that close' and A5's difficulty handing over the ticket concentrate emotional change without unrelated comic decoration.

B4 causes A5: B4 reveals active listening and a mistaken interpretation; A5 revises the meaning of B's silence and hesitates over the ticket.

Alternatives: This version makes renewed inclusion harder; the letter alternative turns painful reinterpretation into a request for B to remain present.

Reservation: B4's interpretation of hearing a name is underexplained, and A5 uses literary phrasing. The scene still supplies a coherent emotional action. No rewrite needed.

## S228-single-b — revised

Carry an unnamed past rupture whose meaning shifts with each new detail, while never letting its emotional weight diminish.

Mechanism: A keeps an unnamed afternoon emotionally present while new details transform its meaning from reaction to prior decision to an unrecognized test.

Confusable concept: Merely uncovering facts would solve a mystery; A instead gives each fact a new relational meaning while continuing to need B's presence.

### before

**A:** I thought your apology was for forgetting my birthday.

**B:** It was for sending your present to the wrong address.

**A:** You remembered, but somebody else got it?

**B:** Your old flat. I was trying to surprise you.

**A:** Now I have to forgive both disappointment and an extremely informed stranger.

### after

**A:** Help me answer this letter. I still can't read your handwriting without going back to that afternoon.

**B:** I wrote it before you arrived.

**A:** Then it wasn't something I said. You'd already decided.

**B:** I left the envelope open because I hoped you'd stop me.

**A:** So what I thought was your decision was a test I didn't know I was taking. Sit with me while I answer.

- cardFidelity: original 2 — A1 names forgetting a birthday and B2 specifies a misdirected gift; A5's forgiveness language also softens the sustained rupture. Result 5 — A1 invokes an unnamed afternoon; A3 recasts the act as premeditated, and A5 recasts it as an undisclosed test.
- responseLogic: original 4 — A3 uses B2's remembered gift and A5 uses B4's old address, but an extremely informed stranger is not established. Result 4 — B2's timing causes A3's prior-decision inference; B4's open-envelope intention causes A5's test interpretation, though the letter's temporal reference is ambiguous.
- relationship: original 3 — B2 and B4 explain an attempted kindness, yet A5 redirects the grievance toward an imagined stranger. Result 4 — B4 reveals hoping A would intervene, and A5 asks B to stay involved rather than simply issuing another accusation.
- heightening: original 3 — A5 adds a recipient to forgive after B4, without clearly deepening the emotional meaning between A and B. Result 4 — A5 adds the burden of an unknowable test to the earlier hurt and makes B's presence part of answering now.
- specificity: original 4 — A1's birthday and B4's old flat provide a concrete delivery mistake, although the recipient remains unknown. Result 4 — A1 supplies handwriting and a letter; B4 supplies an open envelope, but whether B2 refers to this letter or the earlier one is unclear.
- brevityWit: original 3 — A5's extremely informed stranger reaches for a closing joke beyond B4's actual information. Result 4 — A3's 'You'd already decided' and A5's request to sit keep the interpretation tied to immediate interpersonal action.

B4 causes A5: B4 says the open envelope invited intervention; A5 understands the apparent decision as a test A did not know existed.

Alternatives: The letter version uses painful disclosure to seek company; the concert version lets disclosure obstruct a tentative offer of renewed closeness.

Reservation: The relationship between the current letter and the remembered letter needs a consistent performance interpretation. No rewrite required for this internal pass.

## S229-single-a — revised

Treat each compliment as sincere admiration and a new standard you must immediately surpass.

Mechanism: A sincerely accepts each specific compliment as an achieved standard and immediately commits to exceeding it in the next piece of cake work.

Confusable concept: Insecurity that rejects praise would dispute the compliment; A thanks B and treats the admiration as accurate but newly insufficient.

### before

**A:** You called my cake impressive. Now I'll need a second tier before you leave.

**B:** One tier was already impressive.

**A:** Was. We've established that as the baseline.

**B:** You don't have to prove anything.

**A:** Then I'll add a third tier so that becomes convincingly true.

### after

**A:** You called my cake impressive. I'll add a second tier before you leave.

**B:** The icing is perfect already. I came for a slice.

**A:** Thank you. Then the second tier needs icing roses. Perfect can't just happen twice.

**B:** You make those beautifully. Could mine go in a box? My taxi's here.

**A:** Of course. I'll make your rose climb the lid. You should leave with something better than beautiful.

First / second pass: 72 / 96

- cardFidelity: original 4 — A1 and A3 convert admiration into a tier baseline, but A5 responds to B4's reassurance rather than a new compliment. Result 5 — A1 surpasses 'impressive' with another tier, A3 surpasses 'perfect' with roses, and A5 surpasses 'beautifully' with a rose climbing the lid.
- responseLogic: original 3 — A3 directly uses B2's impressive tier; A5's claim that another tier proves nothing needs proving is weakly connected to B4. Result 5 — A3 uses B2's icing praise; A5 combines B4's rose praise, takeaway box and departing taxi into the next embellishment.
- relationship: original 3 — B2 and B4 try to reassure A but offer little concrete pressure or practical alternative. Result 5 — B2 wants a slice and B4 needs to leave, so sincere appreciation repeatedly collides with practical limits.
- heightening: original 3 — A5 adds a third tier, but the numerical increase is less well motivated than A1's second tier. Result 5 — B4's taxi deadline and box request make A5's immediate packaging decoration more consequential than further work on the cake.
- specificity: original 5 — A1 establishes a cake and a visitor about to leave, with visible tiers as a playable target. Result 4 — A1 establishes cake decoration and B4 a waiting taxi, though producing another tier before departure assumes unusually ready materials.
- brevityWit: original 4 — A3's correction from impressive to baseline is economical; A5's paradox is less earned. Result 4 — A3's 'Perfect can't just happen twice' and A5's climbing rose express the escalating standard clearly, with slight explanatory phrasing.

B4 causes A5: B4 praises the roses and asks for a box because the taxi has arrived; A5 makes that box the surface for surpassing the compliment.

Alternatives: This version makes praise obstruct a guest's departure; the office alternative lets the same compulsion become useful help at lunch.

Reservation: The decoration schedule is comically compressed, and A states the standard-setting logic explicitly. No rewrite needed.

## S229-single-b — revised

Treat each compliment as sincere admiration and a new standard you must immediately surpass.

Mechanism: A accepts a colleague's praise as a binding new performance standard and immediately redesigns the next task to surpass it.

Confusable concept: General overwork could continue regardless of feedback; each specific compliment here determines the form of the extra work.

### before

**A:** You said I handled the meeting well. I'll rewrite the whole agenda tonight.

**B:** Why? You were excellent.

**A:** Excellent is now what they'll expect tomorrow.

**B:** You could take a break.

**A:** From a standard you've just raised twice?

### after

**A:** You said I handled the meeting well. I'll improve tomorrow's agenda before lunch.

**B:** Your summary was excellent. Come get a sandwich with me.

**A:** Thank you. I'll make tomorrow's half as long, with clearer decisions.

**B:** You're brilliant at making things simple. Explain the budget to me over lunch.

**A:** Gladly. If simple is my standard, I'll get the whole budget onto your napkin before the sandwiches arrive.

First / second pass: 77 / 93

- cardFidelity: original 4 — A1 and A3 accept praise as a higher standard, but A5 declines a break without specifying an immediate further improvement. Result 5 — A1 responds to praise with agenda improvement, A3 raises the summary standard, and A5 turns simplicity praise into a napkin-sized budget challenge.
- responseLogic: original 4 — A3 directly uses B2's excellent rating; A5 answers B4's break offer by referring back to the two earlier compliments. Result 5 — B2's excellent summary causes A3's shorter, clearer version; B4's budget request over lunch determines A5's format and deadline.
- relationship: original 4 — B2 affirms A's success and B4 offers rest, giving B a credible caring position. Result 5 — B2 wants A to join lunch, and B4 finds a useful request that channels A's improvement drive into companionship.
- heightening: original 3 — A5 restates the raised-standard problem after B4 rather than developing A1's proposed agenda rewrite. Result 4 — A5 commits to the whole budget on one napkin before food arrives, increasing compression and urgency after B4's request.
- specificity: original 4 — A1 establishes meeting performance and an agenda, but A3 does not explain what would surpass excellence. Result 4 — A1 establishes meeting preparation and B2 sandwiches; A5 supplies a playable napkin task, although the budget's contents remain unspecified.
- brevityWit: original 4 — A5's counted compliments make an efficient response, although the final turn summarizes more than acts. Result 4 — A5's napkin and sandwich deadline earn the comic ambition from B4's lunch request, though 'If simple is my standard' explains the mechanism.

B4 causes A5: B4 praises simplicity and requests a budget explanation at lunch; A5 exceeds that standard through napkin compression and a food-arrival deadline.

Alternatives: B successfully redirects the behavior into shared lunch and useful explanation; the cake guest's attempts to leave generate additional decoration.

Reservation: A5 explains its standard-setting rationale more than necessary, but the helpful interaction and concrete constraint sustain it. No rewrite needed.
