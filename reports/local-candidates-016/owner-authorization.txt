7. COACH-2.0: UNDERSTANDABLE SCENE LOGIC FIRST
============================================================

Use five spoken turns: A–B–A–B–A.

No narrator paragraphs, gray action blocks, or abstract coaching
inside the player-facing example.

A1 establishes an immediate interaction.
B2 contributes something plausible.
A3 uses B2 through the selected card behavior.
B4 changes the pressure, options, or understanding.
A5 uses B4 to develop the same underlying behavior.

These are conversational functions, not stock sentence slots.

Apply each gate as PASS, FAIL, or UNCLEAR with specific evidence.
UNCLEAR does not qualify as writer-ready.

G1 — LEGIBLE INTERACTION

Can the reader understand what these people are doing and why
this immediate exchange matters?

Separate:
- Facts stated in the dialogue.
- Reasonable ordinary inference.
- Essential information that remains unestablished.

Do not invent backstory to rescue unclear dialogue.
Do not demand irrelevant names, occupations, or biographies.

G2 — COHERENT FACTS AND KNOWLEDGE

Track material objects, ownership, timing, responsibility,
authority, and what each character knows.

New information must enter intelligibly.
Recognizable lies, revelations, and misunderstandings are allowed.
Accidental contradictions and unprepared prerequisites are not.

Do not justify ambiguity with “staging can clarify” when the reader
only receives dialogue.

G3 — ALL FOUR TRANSITIONS CONNECT

Examine:
A1→B2
B2→A3
A3→B4
B4→A5

Identify what each reply answers, uses, changes, or deliberately
avoids. Shared nouns are not sufficient evidence.

Purposeful evasion can be the behavior. Accidental topic changes
cannot be excused as evasion after the fact.

G4 — B IS A PLAUSIBLE PARTICIPANT

B has a credible want, reaction, condition, or practical aim.

Would B plausibly say this in the situation without knowing that
the writer needs a setup for A's ending?

B may help, agree, bargain, correct, reveal, concede, or resist.
B must affect A rather than merely cue prepared lines.

G5 — BOTH EXACT CARDS CAUSE A'S CHOICES

Identify the distinct contribution of each instruction.

The Stance changes HOW A pursues the Drive in one interaction.
B responds to that combined behavior.

If either card can be removed without losing an important reason
for A's tactic or reaction, investigate whether it is decorative.

Do not demand exclusive correspondence with only one possible card.
Do demand meaningful correspondence with both selected cards.

G6 — THE ENDING IS EARNED

A5 uses B4 to develop the established behavior.

Development may involve a concession, vulnerability, changed
status, a practical consequence, or accepting help on revealing
terms. It need not become more hostile or obstructive.

Reject detachable jokes and convenient new rules, people, or
objects introduced solely to manufacture an ending.

FORMAT GATE

Five spoken ABABA turns within the actual supported word budget
and source schema. No sixth turn, narration, or silent truncation.

The character's priorities may be irrational.
The reader must still understand the reasoning.
