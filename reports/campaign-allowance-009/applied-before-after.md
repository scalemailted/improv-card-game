## S130-single-a — revised

Treat each clue and inconsistency as a contest over who can understand the situation first and most completely.

Mechanism: A treats understanding each clue first and extending that understanding furthest as a competition.

Confusable concept: Credit seeking wants recognition for a contribution; this card makes continuing interpretation itself the race.

### before

**A:** I've found the missing parcel's tracking error. Put my name beside it.

**B:** We were both looking.

**A:** I circled it while you were saying looking.

**B:** Let's fix it together.

**A:** After recording who made together useful.

### after

**A:** I've solved your missing parcel. Tracking says reception; you were still refreshing.

**B:** Reception signed for three boxes. Only two reached our office.

**A:** Then one stayed downstairs. I've narrowed your whole building to one desk.

**B:** The trolley's here. There's a box wedged underneath.

**A:** So it left reception but never got unloaded. You've found the parcel; I've solved why we've been calling reception all morning.

First / second pass: 66 / 86

- cardFidelity: original 3 — A1 and A3 claim credit for an error, but A5 prioritizes recording credit over understanding more completely. Result 4 — A1 contrasts A's claimed solution with B 'still refreshing'; A3 claims superior narrowing; A5 counters B's discovery by claiming the more complete explanation. Competitive understanding is clear, though A3's certainty outruns the evidence.
- responseLogic: original 4 — A3 counters B2's shared search; A5 makes B4's cooperative fix conditional on attribution. Result 5 — B2's three-box receipt and two-box arrival cause A3's downstairs hypothesis. B4's box under the trolley overturns that hypothesis, and A5 incorporates it into an unloading explanation while reclaiming credit.
- relationship: original 4 — B2 claims joint effort and B4 offers practical cooperation that A obstructs. Result 4 — B2 contributes delivery facts and B4 actually finds the box, pursuing parcel recovery rather than merely feeding boasts. A5 acknowledges B's achievement while competing over what counts as solving.
- heightening: original 2 — A5 repeats the credit demand without introducing another clue or a more complete explanation. Result 4 — After B4 wins the physical search, A5 raises the contest from locating the parcel to explaining the morning's wasted calls. This develops the established competition, though chiefly through a verbal claim.
- specificity: original 4 — A1 establishes a missing parcel's tracking error and an immediate attribution dispute. Result 5 — A1 immediately establishes parcel tracking and reception; B2 supplies the three-versus-two discrepancy; B4 reveals the box wedged under the trolley. Each detail gives actors a clear, playable discovery.
- brevityWit: original 3 — A3's circled-versus-looking line fits rivalry; A5's together-useful phrasing becomes abstract. Result 4 — A3's 'one desk' makes premature certainty compactly comic. A5's distinction between finding and solving follows B4, although the final explanation is longer and more explicit than the earlier boasts.

B4 causes A5: B4 locates the box under the trolley, forcing A5 to abandon the downstairs claim and compete instead over explaining why it remained missing.

Alternatives: The preserved S130-single-b has B challenge successive mystery-judge deductions while A claims the fuller sequence. Here B independently recovers a real missing item, forcing A to concede the discovery and shift the criterion for victory to explanation.

Reservation: A3 makes an unsupported deduction, and A5's explanatory victory is slight because B4 has supplied the decisive evidence. These remain playable expressions of sincere competitive certainty. No rewrite is needed.
