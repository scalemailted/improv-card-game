# v0.25.0 — Reviewed pair reference scenes

These 25 pairs / 50 scenes are internally rewritten examples. A holds both cards. B is an illustrative partner, not a direction to the actual other performer. This status does not extend to the compiled pair corpus.

## S67+D101 — Chain of Command + Start the Hard Part

**Chain of Command:** Treat every question, favor, and decision as something that must travel through the proper hierarchy.

**Start the Hard Part:** Get someone else to explain, announce, or begin the emotionally difficult part.

**Fusion:** The choir cancellation and house sale are Drive seeds; delegating the announcement up the authority chain embodies the Stance.

### Scene 1

**A:** You chair the choir. You announce the cancelled tour; I'll distribute the refund forms.  
**B:** You're the organiser who cancelled it.  
**A:** Then my report must travel upward before the bad news travels outward.  
**B:** You could simply tell them.  
**A:** Not before the chair introduces the emotionally difficult item. I'm ready with the forms.  

### Scene 2

**A:** As eldest, you should tell Mum we're selling the house.  
**B:** You're the one who wants to sell it.  
**A:** The proposal originated below you. The announcement requires seniority.  
**B:** You can't hide behind my birthday.  
**A:** I'm respecting the family hierarchy. You say 'selling'; I'll explain the plumbing afterwards.  

## S192+D74 — Everything Is Policy + Stay Until We’re Okay

**Everything Is Policy:** Treat every personal choice as evidence of a larger system. Connect exceptions, habits, and coincidences until they become institutional doctrine.

**Stay Until We’re Okay:** Keep someone present until the relationship feels repaired, even if the practical reason for staying disappears.

**Fusion:** The Stance turns ordinary recurring personal choices into policy; A uses this policy specifically to delay departure until repair is explicit.

### Scene 1

**A:** You always make tea after we argue. Shall we finish the reconciliation procedure before your taxi?  
**B:** I've made the tea. We're done.  
**A:** The tea initiates the procedure. Agreement closes it.  
**B:** What agreement?  
**A:** That you want tomorrow's tea with me too. Then I can authorise the taxi.  

### Scene 2

**A:** You usually say 'see you tomorrow' before leaving. Is its absence a policy change?  
**B:** I'm just tired.  
**A:** Good. We'll record fatigue as an exception, not permanent withdrawal.  
**B:** Can I go now?  
**A:** After confirming the exception still includes seeing me tomorrow.  

## S01+D13 — Top of the Ladder + Helpful Culprit

**Top of the Ladder:** Carry yourself as the highest-status person present. Treat questions as requests for your approval.

**Helpful Culprit:** Hide that the problem is your fault while enthusiastically helping investigate it.

**Fusion:** A turns the exact culprit investigation into a status performance, claiming the authority to assess incriminating evidence.

### Scene 1

**A:** I've taken charge of the broken-vase investigation. Questions require my approval.  
**B:** Why is there glue on your hand?  
**A:** Approved question. I'll appoint myself lead investigator of the glue.  
**B:** There's a piece of vase stuck to you.  
**A:** Excellent work. You're promoted to observing other people while I preserve this evidence.  

### Scene 2

**A:** I've authorised an inquiry into the dead office plant.  
**B:** You watered it with coffee.  
**A:** Then we'll investigate the coffee machine under my supervision.  
**B:** You made the coffee too.  
**A:** Useful connection. I'll decide how much weight to give your promising theory.  

## S22+D14 — Practical Realist + Not That Subject

**Practical Realist:** Accept any unusual premise without debate. Focus immediately on schedules, costs, safety, and what must happen next.

**Not That Subject:** Choose one subject you cannot let the scene reach. Redirect whenever the conversation gets close.

**Fusion:** The talking ceiling and dragon retain unusual-premise acceptance; logistical questions explicitly divert from the damage or expense A is avoiding.

### Scene 1

**A:** Fine, the ceiling predicts floods. Before discussing who broke the pipe, what's its maintenance budget?  
**B:** Did you break the pipe?  
**A:** First we need somewhere dry for its microphone. Prophecy carries poorly over running water.  
**B:** You're avoiding the question.  
**A:** I'm prioritising electrics. Who insured a talking ceiling against its own predictions?  

### Scene 2

**A:** Your dragon can inspect the rent letter later. Which meter covers its fire?  
**B:** The letter says you spent the rent.  
**A:** Then the dragon's gas contribution is suddenly very relevant.  
**B:** On curtains, apparently.  
**A:** Flame-resistant curtains? We should check that before opening another financial question.

### Scene 2

**B:** What drain?  
**A:** I stacked every chair. I deserve more than the public summary.  
**B:** You're supplying details, not requesting them.  
**A:** Then perhaps I should help decide which details remain confidential.

## S144+D126 — Competing Theories + What Really Happened?

**Competing Theories:** Create several explanations for what is happening. Test each new clue against them, and switch allegiance whenever one theory gains strength.

**What Really Happened?:** Find the truth by comparing details, repeating questions, and testing which parts of the story remain consistent.

**Fusion:** Competing explanations guide exact evidence questions; new details force A to shift theory rather than stack unconnected accusations.

### Scene 1

**A:** Was the missing cake taken, dropped, or eaten by the dog? Describe the crumbs before choosing.  
**B:** There's icing on the dog.

**B:** Yes, after you fed it your slice.  
**A:** Then theft returns to contention. Start again with who opened the cupboard.  

### Scene 2

**A:** The parcel was lost, stolen, or delivered elsewhere. Was it a bag or a box?  
**B:** A box with handles.  
**A:** Delivery error leads. Describe the lid.  
**B:** It was tied, not hinged.  
**A:** Then 'box' loses support. Let's establish the container before accusing a driver.
