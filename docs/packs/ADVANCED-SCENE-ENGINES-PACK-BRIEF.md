# Imprompt Pack Brief — Advanced Scene Engines

**Pack ID:** `advanced-scene-engines`  
**Reserved IDs:** S217–S240 and D217–D240  
**Target release:** Imprompt v0.17.0  
**In-app status:** Playtest  
**Candidate pool:** 64 cards drafted; 48 selected; 8 held; 8 rejected

## Creative promise

**Advanced Scene Engines makes the scene remember, contradict, and transform itself.** The pack is designed for experienced improvisers who can hold more than one active idea without abandoning their partner. Its cards create structure through callbacks, delayed reveals, earned reversals, recurring details, double binds, changing meanings, and consequences that make earlier offers matter more later.

The prompts do not demand clever plotting or a predetermined twist. Each one gives the holder an immediate playable behavior and a second structural pressure that can emerge over time. A useful Advanced Scene Engines card should make the present moment playable while quietly creating material the scene can return to.

## Central comic question

> **How can one performer make earlier choices gain new meaning without abandoning the shared scene?**

## What makes this pack distinct

- **Core Foundations** supplies broad, immediately accessible scene fundamentals.
- **Everyday Friction** personalizes ordinary inconvenience.
- **Power Games** explores authority and leverage.
- **Relationship Knots** explores attachment and unfinished connection.
- **Emotional Pressure** reorganizes behavior around feeling.
- **Secrets & Schemes** makes information and concealment active.
- **Absurd Commitment** builds coherent impossible logic.
- **Rules, Rituals & Institutions** makes systems and customs playable.
- **Competition & Consequences** makes outcomes, bargains, and accountability matter.
- **Advanced Scene Engines** uses contradiction, return, reversal, and recontextualization to shape the scene across time.

An Advanced Scene Engines card should not merely be difficult. It should create an **earned structural relationship** between earlier and later moments.

## Included territory

- callbacks whose meaning changes when they return;
- details planted lightly and paid off responsively;
- simultaneous truths, needs, or emotional directions;
- reversals of meaning, status, allegiance, or apparent objective;
- delayed disclosure that recontextualizes rather than invalidates;
- repeated language, actions, objects, and relationship patterns;
- foreshadowing created from offers already present in the scene;
- small choices that become unavoidable consequences;
- perspective shifts that preserve continuity;
- experienced performers using structure while remaining fully responsive.

## Excluded territory

This pack should not rely on:

- refusing or contradicting every offer;
- arbitrary character changes with no established cause;
- fixed twists such as “it was all a dream” or “you were dead”;
- ignoring the partner while privately planning a reveal;
- forcing another performer to remember, forget, confess, or react in one exact way;
- stockpiling details without playing the present moment;
- audience response as an in-world objective;
- complicated rules that cannot be understood in approximately five seconds;
- callbacks that merely repeat wording without changing emotional or practical meaning;
- reversals that erase rather than deepen prior choices.

## Portability rule

The card defines a structural behavior, not a scripted plot.

Too narrow:

> Reveal halfway through that the missing suitcase contains the mayor’s twin.

Portable:

> **Reveal the Truth in Layers** — Protect someone by disclosing the truth in stages, changing what each earlier statement appears to mean.

The portable version can function in any genre, location, or relationship and allows the partner’s offers to determine the actual content.

## Hidden-information rule

A private advanced prompt may guide what the holder plants, withholds, repeats, or reinterprets. It cannot make the unseen card a secret script the partner must correctly guess.

The holder should:

1. make an immediate playable offer;
2. remain open to the partner’s interpretation;
3. treat resistance or misunderstanding as useful material;
4. earn later structure from what the scene actually establishes;
5. prefer a discovered callback over a preplanned twist.

## Desired player experience

A player should quickly understand:

1. the first observable behavior;
2. the detail, contradiction, or pressure that can return;
3. how the engine can heighten without becoming arbitrary;
4. how the partner can influence the meaning of the eventual turn;
5. how failure to achieve the private objective still creates scene;
6. how to preserve emotional truth while using visible structure.

## Metadata additions

This pack adds six reusable coach roles:

`scene-architect`, `callback-builder`, `reversal-maker`, `delayed-revealer`, `double-binder`, and `pattern-weaver`.

It also adds controlled motifs:

`ambiguity`, `callback`, `contradiction`, `delay`, `delayed-reveal`, `double-bind`, `echo`, `escalation`, `foreshadowing`, `perspective`, `recontextualization`, `repetition`, `reversal`, `structure`, `subtext`, `symmetry`, and `turning-point`.

These remain internal metadata. The visible seven-category card system does not change.

## Exercise value

Advanced Scene Engines materially expands:

- **Status Clash**, through ambiguous rulings, blind spots, reversals, and fragile authority callbacks;
- **Crown & Heart**, through double binds between control, care, belonging, and recognition;
- **Past & Future**, through recurring memories whose meaning changes over time;
- **Pursuer & Avoider**, through layered disclosure, indirect inquiry, and delayed confession pressure;
- **Absurd Commitment**, through recurring rituals, practical consequences, and coherent pattern returns;
- **Instigator & Anchor**, through choices that establish one reality and later expose another;
- custom veteran workshops centered on callbacks, reversals, subtext, foreshadowing, double binds, or scene architecture.

## Live-playtest questions

1. Can players begin each card immediately without waiting to engineer a twist?
2. Do callbacks arise from shared details rather than private planning?
3. Does each reversal deepen established reality instead of replacing it?
4. Can partners misunderstand the structural move without making the card fail?
5. Do double-bind cards create playable tension rather than indecision?
6. Are delayed reveals emotionally earned and concise?
7. Does the pack reward listening more than cleverness?
8. Can the cards work in grounded scenes as well as heightened ones?
9. Which prompts create satisfying postmortems about how meaning changed?
10. Which cards are too cognitively demanding for ordinary mixed-experience play?
