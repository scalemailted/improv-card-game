## S238-single-a — revised

Accept every twist, but keep returning to one practical consequence whose importance grows as the scene becomes more complicated.

Mechanism: A accepts multiplying magical fountains while repeatedly recalculating the growing practical consequence of water charged to the same household.

Confusable concept: Generic practical skepticism objects to magic; this card accepts the magic and tracks one continuing liability as its scale increases.

### before

**A:** Fine, the fountain grants wishes. Who pays its water bill?

**B:** It can wish the water free.

**A:** Then who pays the sewer charge?

**B:** It has turned the pipes to gold.

**A:** Lovely. Does the same meter now charge us for gold drainage?

### after

**A:** Our garden fountain grants wishes. Fine. We're still paying for its water.

**B:** I wished for ten more. We can sell admission.

**A:** Eleven fountains on our meter. Charge enough to cover that.

**B:** Visitors wished for fountains at home. They're all fed from ours.

**A:** Then we're paying to water the whole queue's gardens. Sell them monthly passes before the next bill arrives.

- responseLogic: original 4 — B2's free water causes A3's sewer question; B4's gold pipes cause A5's gold-drainage question, though pipe material need not change the charge. Result 5 — B2's ten additional fountains and admission proposal produce A3's eleven-fountain pricing concern. B4's shared supply produces A5's recurring-pass proposal.
- relationship: original 4 — B2 and B4 offer increasingly extravagant solutions while A maintains a credible concern about shared costs. Result 3 — B2 wants admission income and A3 collaborates on pricing, but B4 mainly supplies another magical complication without much personal response to its cost.
- heightening: original 4 — A5 worries that B4's valuable plumbing complicates the same ongoing bill rather than removing it. Result 4 — B4 expands their metered supply into visitors' gardens, causing A5 to replace simple admission with ongoing payments before the bill arrives.

- brevityWit: original 4 — A3's sewer-charge follow-up earns its mundanity by accepting B2's free-water solution. Result 4 — A3's immediate fountain arithmetic and A5's subscription response make financial housekeeping the consistent comic reaction to magic.

B4 causes A5: B4 establishes that visitors' home fountains use their supply; A5 recognises the ongoing cost and proposes monthly passes.

Alternatives: The preserved time-travelling house alternative tracks a leak through escalating physical danger. This candidate tracks utility costs through a cooperative attempt to commercialise magic.

## S238-single-b — revised

Accept every twist, but keep returning to one practical consequence whose importance grows as the scene becomes more complicated.

Mechanism: A accepts extraordinary changes of location while repeatedly prioritizing one roof leak as its consequences become more urgent.

Confusable concept: A recurring complaint can stay static; this card requires the same practical concern to gain importance as complications accumulate.

### before

**A:** I accept that the house travels through time. The roof still leaks.

**B:** We've arrived in ancient Rome.

**A:** Good. Ask whether someone here repairs tiles.

**B:** Now we're at the moon landing.

**A:** Then put the original bucket under the original leak before it floats away.

### after

**A:** Fine, our house travels through time. Can we stop somewhere that fixes roof tiles?

**B:** Ancient Rome. I'll find a roofer.

**A:** Get a quote. The bucket's already half full.

**B:** I touched the dial. We're underwater now.

**A:** Right. That same leak is admitting the sea. Reverse us while I empty the bucket into the sink.

First / second pass: 70 / 95

- cardFidelity: original 4 — A1 accepts time travel and A3 and A5 return to the leak, but its growing importance depends on A5's unsupported floating bucket. Result 5 — A1 accepts time travel while prioritizing roof tiles; A3 monitors the bucket; A5 keeps the same leak central when it admits the sea.
- responseLogic: original 3 — A3 uses B2's Rome for tile repairs; B4's moon landing does not establish that a bucket inside the house floats away. Result 5 — B2's proposed Roman roofer prompts A3's quote request; B4's underwater mistake makes A5 demand reversal while managing the leak.
- relationship: original 3 — B2 and B4 announce destinations without explaining any travel goal or response to A's repair request. Result 4 — B2 helps seek repair and B4 admits a control error, giving A both a collaborator and someone able to reverse the danger.
- heightening: original 3 — A5 adds bucket loss, but B4 does not clearly cause that practical complication. Result 5 — A5 turns A3's half-full bucket from a repair nuisance into an inadequate response to the sea entering the same hole.
- specificity: original 4 — A1's leaking time-travelling house and A3's tile repair request provide concrete objects and tasks. Result 5 — A1's roof tiles, A3's bucket and B4's dial provide clear objects and practical tasks within the fantastic situation.
- brevityWit: original 4 — A3's immediate search for a Roman roofer follows the established practical priority economically. Result 4 — A3's quote request keeps the fantastic journey domestic; A5's bucket-to-sink response earns absurdity from the established repair concern.

B4 causes A5: B4 places the house underwater through a dial mistake; A5 identifies the same leak's increased danger and asks B to reverse it.

Alternatives: Alternative b develops a shared repair effort into urgent coordination; a relies on repeated hypothetical billing questions.

Reservation: No rewrite needed. Emptying into the sink is deliberately inadequate; its playability depends on A pursuing it sincerely.
