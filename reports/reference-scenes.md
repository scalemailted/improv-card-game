# Act-It-Out reference scenes · v0.24.0

These are 25 bespoke pairs (50 examples) included in the package. Their presence does not certify the remaining composed pairs. All single source material remains available in examples/authoring/single-scenes.json.

## Chain of Command + Start the Hard Part

**S67:** Treat every question, favor, and decision as something that must travel through the proper hierarchy.

**D101:** Get someone else to explain, announce, or begin the emotionally difficult part.

### Example 1 · ABA

**A** [I slide the cancellation notice up the committee chart.] “The chair introduces difficult business. You tell them the trip is cancelled; I will take the minutes.”

**B** “You cancelled the trip.”

**A** “Which makes me the author of the notice, not its authorized speaker.”

### Example 2 · ABABA

**A** [I put my name under Secretary, leaving Chair blank.] “I have volunteered to record the difficult conversation.”

**B** “Then who starts it?”

**A** [I push the blank Chair badge toward you.] “The highest-ranking person. Congratulations on your promotion.”

**B** “Can I decline?”

**A** “Absolutely. The deputy would then inherit the announcement. I have left that badge available too.”

## Everything Is Policy + Stay Until We’re Okay

**S192:** Treat every personal choice as evidence of a larger system. Connect exceptions, habits, and coincidences until they become institutional doctrine.

**D74:** Keep someone present until the relationship feels repaired, even if the practical reason for staying disappears.

### Example 1 · ABA

**A** [I hold my coat and open a form headed Friendship Closure.] “Initial here to confirm we have made up. Then I can leave.”

**B** “I already said we were fine.”

**A** “That was verbal approval. We still need the written version to match the tone.”

### Example 2 · ABABA

**A** [I number the two empty teacups.] “We cannot end the visit until both parties approve the final tea.”

**B** “I finished mine.”

**A** [I refill my own cup.] “A refill reopens the procedure. Are we definitely okay?”

**B** “You are inventing rules to stay.”

**A** “Then let us record that understanding together before closing the visit.”

## Top of the Ladder + Helpful Culprit

**S01:** Carry yourself as the highest-status person present. Treat questions as requests for your approval.

**D13:** Hide that the problem is your fault while enthusiastically helping investigate it.

### Example 1 · ABA

**A** [I hide my glue-covered thumb behind an evidence clipboard.] “I will lead the broken-vase inquiry. Start by examining everyone who was outside.”

**B** “You were alone with the vase.”

**A** “Exactly why I am best placed to decide which details matter.”

### Example 2 · ABABA

**A** [I cover my muddy footprint with a sign reading Investigation Headquarters.] “All evidence comes through me. Nobody moves this sign.”

**B** “It is covering your boot print.”

**A** [I put a second sign over my boot.] “An excellent clue. I am appointing myself evidence custodian.”

**B** “Can someone else examine it?”

**A** “Once my office approves an independent review of my office.”

## Practical Realist + Not That Subject

**S22:** Accept any unusual premise without debate. Focus immediately on schedules, costs, safety, and what must happen next.

**D14:** Choose one subject you cannot let the scene reach. Redirect whenever the conversation gets close.

### Example 1 · ABA

**A** [I cover the signature on the haunted copier invoice with a calculator.] “Fine, it prints prophecies. Before we discuss who ordered it, what is its maintenance budget?”

**B** “Your name is on the order.”

**A** “Then invoice me after we decide whether prophecy ink counts as a consumable.”

### Example 2 · ABABA

**A** [I slide my summoning note under the dragon feeding chart.] “Yes, there is a dragon in reception. Who is buying enough breakfast?”

**B** “Did you summon it?”

**A** [I count the empty cereal boxes.] “We can discuss authorship after somebody takes the first feeding shift.”

**B** “You keep changing the subject.”

**A** “Only toward the hungry part of it. Is there a bulk discount?”

## Cleared to Know + How Much Do You Know?

**S124:** Treat access to private information as proof that you belong, and exclusion from any detail as a challenge to your legitimacy.

**D06:** Probe for what others know while revealing as little as possible.

### Example 1 · ABA

**A** [I turn my visitor badge facedown beside the closed folder.] “You can give me the insider version. Which account of last night have you heard?”

**B** “Which account did you hear?”

**A** “I want to know whether we have the same clearance before comparing notes.”

### Example 2 · ABABA

**A** [I pull up a chair at the private briefing.] “Start with the part everyone in this room knows.”

**B** “Tell us your part first.”

**A** [I open an empty notebook as though checking classified notes.] “I would hate to reveal something above your clearance.”

**B** “Are you actually cleared?”

**A** “An excellent question. Which details would a cleared person already have?”

## Don’t Pity Me + Let Me Lean

**S114:** Frame your struggle as evidence of strength, steering concern toward admiration and rejecting anything that feels like pity.

**D97:** Get someone to carry part of the emotional burden without making you name how heavy it has become.

### Example 1 · ABA

**A** [I put the unopened result letter beside my medal.] “Read the first line for me. As a colleague, not as someone you feel sorry for.”

**B** “I can read all of it.”

**A** “One line. I am delegating a task, not surrendering my entire competence.”

### Example 2 · ABABA

**A** [I slide my ringing phone across and start polishing the medal.] “Take this call. Consider it a temporary exchange of professional duties.”

**B** “You look upset.”

**A** [I set the polishing cloth down precisely.] “I look like someone assigning a phone call.”

**B** “What can I tell them?”

**A** “That I will answer the hard part myself after a very competent minute.”

## The Important Thing + Raise the Impossibility

**S23:** Choose one ordinary object, phrase, or rule and treat it as vitally important. Keep bringing the scene back to it.

**D163:** Answer every strange offer with a more committed version that you can still justify.

### Example 1 · ABA

**A** [I steady the single floating chair with both hands.] “This chair is everything. A flying chair needs a flying table.”

**B** “The whole house is floating.”

**A** “The house can wait. Where does this chair put its breakfast?”

### Example 2 · ABABA

**A** [I tie a ribbon around the floating chair to mark it as essential.] “Nobody touches this. It will need a flying dining room.”

**B** “Could we tether the house first?”

**A** [I sketch a ceiling above the chair.] “Only if that gives my chair a stable sky.”

**B** “Now you want a sky?”

**A** “A small one. Everything else can use the ordinary weather.”

## Sacred Procedure + Don’t Go

**S19:** Treat an ordinary activity as a solemn ritual. Protect every step, object, and tradition from casual interference.

**D02:** Create reasons another player must stay with you. Never directly ask them to stay.

### Example 1 · ABA

**A** [I turn the teaspoon three times as you reach for your coat.] “We have not performed the proper goodbye. Hold the biscuit level.”

**B** “There. Can I go now?”

**A** “After the witness biscuit confirms it. I will fetch another plate.”

### Example 2 · ABABA

**A** [I set two cups in a farewell circle.] “The guest leaves after the closing tea.”

**B** “I have finished mine.”

**A** [I refill both cups solemnly.] “You finished before the farewell toast. We have to restart together.”

**B** “Is there a short version?”

**A** “Yes. We should discuss which shortened ceremony would honour this visit.”

## Replacement Fear + Give It Up

**S15:** Behave as if your place is insecure. Make yourself indispensable and treat small changes as threats.

**D12:** Persuade someone to surrender something they value.

### Example 1 · ABA

**A** [I remove the new worker’s nameplate and hold out my keyring.] “Give me the master key. You would miss too many locked cupboards without me.”

**B** “The new worker needs access too.”

**A** “Then they can ask me. That keeps the indispensable part of the job in place.”

### Example 2 · ABABA

**A** [I bring the only labelled spare-key box to my chair.] “Hand over the spare set. I know every lock.”

**B** “We could label another set.”

**A** [I close the box.] “Labels cannot remember which doors stick when it rains.”

**B** “We can write that down.”

**A** “Then give me the notebook as well. The institutional memory should remain together.”

## Their Opinion Matters + Recognize Me

**S17:** Seek one person's approval while pretending their opinion is irrelevant. Adjust yourself whenever they respond.

**D09:** Make someone acknowledge your importance, expertise, or authority.

### Example 1 · ABA

**A** [I move my name higher on the repair certificate while watching your face.] “Say who fixed it. Unless that sounds boastful; I can use smaller letters.”

**B** “You fixed it.”

**A** [I choose a smaller pen.] “Good. Was that admiration or just factual accuracy?”

### Example 2 · ABABA

**A** [I hold up the certificate, then lower it when you blink.] “Could you introduce me as the person who solved this? Casually.”

**B** “How casually?”

**A** [I demonstrate an over-rehearsed shrug.] “With the amount of enthusiasm you think I merit.”

**B** “You want me to choose the praise?”

**A** “Purely for accuracy. I can adjust the shrug afterward.”

**A** [I open my backup kit with a delighted gasp.] “An authentic practice opportunity! Use the spare exit.”

**A** “Then we can test the full emergency plan at last.”

## The Responsible One + Make It Their Idea

**S09:** Take responsibility for everyone and everything. Fix problems before anyone asks, then resent having to do it.

**D05:** Make an unwanted task sound appealing enough that someone volunteers for it.

### Example 1 · ABA

**A** [I sort every cup except one enormous pan.] “I have handled the ordinary cleaning. Who wants the special finishing assignment?”

**B** “That pan needs scrubbing.”

**A** “Exactly. I thought someone might enjoy finally being trusted with something important.”

### Example 2 · ABABA

**A** [I polish the mop handle and present it like an award.] “I have prepared everything for our first floor-care specialist.”

**B** “Why don’t you do it?”

**A** [I point to my completed chore list.] “Because I believe in giving other people opportunities I have already had twelve times.”

**B** “Does the specialist get anything?”

**A** “My sincere relief, which you may interpret as enthusiastic support.”

## Competing Theories + What Really Happened?

**S144:** Create several explanations for what is happening. Test each new clue against them, and switch allegiance whenever one theory gains strength.

**D126:** Find the truth by comparing details, repeating questions, and testing which parts of the story remain consistent.

### Example 1 · ABA

**A** [I put the two accounts under Accident and Deliberate.] “The missing ten minutes could support either. Tell me what happened before the lights went out.”

**B** “I was looking for the switch.”

**A** “That helps Accident. Which switch did you try first?”

### Example 2 · ABABA

**A** [I move the bag photograph from one theory to another.] “You said box before. Describe the handles without naming the container.”

**B** “There were two straps.”

**A** [I cross out the box theory.] “Then the bag survives. Where did those straps leave marks?”

**B** “You’ve changed your explanation.”

**A** “Good. I would rather lose a theory than keep inventing support for it.”

## The Crown Must Pass + Pass Me the Authority

**S53:** Treat another person as your likely successor. Prepare them to lead, then reclaim authority whenever their independence makes your influence feel temporary.

**D60:** Get someone to transfer authority publicly by naming you as the person who decides what happens next.

### Example 1 · ABA

**A** [I pass you the chair’s badge but keep the appeals file.] “You’re chair now. Announce that I’m the final authority on appeals.”

**B** “Then who actually decides?”

**A** “You decide. I decide which decisions stand.”

### Example 2 · ABABA

**A** [I hand you the keys and put on a badge marked adviser.] “The place is yours. Just introduce me as the person whose advice cannot be ignored.”

**B** “Is that advice or an order?”

**A** “Advice. You’re in charge of following it.”

**B** “And when I disagree?”

**A** “We’ll announce how independently you requested a second opinion.”

## Don’t Break the Routine + Try It First

**S43:** Protect a familiar routine as though changing one step would invite chaos into everything else.

**D32:** Get someone else to test the shortcut, substitute, or questionable fix before you rely on it.

### Example 1 · ABA

**A** [I keep the morning mugs in their usual order and hand you the repaired tap handle.] “Test the replacement first. If it works, breakfast can proceed exactly as always.”

**B** “Couldn’t we use bottled water?”

**A** “That changes the kettle step. I’d rather risk your test than our entire routine.”

### Example 2 · ABABA

**A** [I set the experimental fitting in the familiar teacup position.] “Try it once before I move anything else.”

**B** “It might leak.”

**A** [I put a towel under your cup, leaving mine untouched.] “Then we will learn whether the old order can survive the new part.”

**B** “Why me first?”

**A** “Someone must remain available to preserve the original breakfast sequence.”

## Old Reflex + What Are We Now?

**S83:** Turn to this person first whenever uncertainty appears, as an old reflex. Notice the habit only after you have already involved them.

**D78:** Find out how someone defines the relationship without directly asking for a label.

### Example 1 · ABA

**A** [I hand you my keys automatically, then pause over the invitation.] “What do I put after your name? My hands still seem to think you’re my person.”

**B** “Just put friend.”

**A** “All right. Which friend gets handed my keys without either of us asking?”

### Example 2 · ABABA

**A** [I begin dialling your number while you stand beside me.] “I was going to ask you where we should sit at the wedding.”

**B** “You can ask me now.”

**A** [I show the seating form.] “As what? I keep reaching for you before I know the label.”

**B** “We don’t need a label.”

**A** “Then help me fill in the box without pretending you’re anybody else.”

## Waiting for the Bad News + Hope with Me

**S111:** Treat pauses, changes, and uncertainty as signs that rejection or loss is approaching, then prepare yourself before it arrives.

**D103:** Recruit someone into believing that the outcome can still change, even when the evidence is thin.

### Example 1 · ABA

**A** [I pack an emergency box beside our optimistic route map.] “Help me plan the good outcome. I want something to hope for while preparing for the worst.”

**B** “Why pack for failure?”

**A** “So it won’t interrupt us if hope gets a chance to work. Draw one route that stays open.”

### Example 2 · ABABA

**A** [I place the celebration glasses inside a protective packing box.] “Let’s plan the toast before the answer arrives.”

**B** “You think they’ll reject us.”

**A** [I pad the glasses more carefully.] “I can expect bad news and still ask you to hope with me.”

**B** “What should we toast?”

**A** “The possibility I haven’t managed to pack away yet.”

## What Do You Need Now? + Ready on My Terms

**S118:** Accept every feeling as real, then translate it into water, space, a plan, a boundary, or the next practical step.

**D119:** Agree to be honest, hopeful, or vulnerable only in forms that preserve your emotional safety.

### Example 1 · ABA

**A** [I set water beside the unopened results and choose the chair by the door.] “I can talk about the first page today. Water first, then that page.”

**B** “And the rest?”

**A** “Another day with another glass. One manageable piece is still honest.”

### Example 2 · ABABA

**A** [I put a tissue box between two chairs.] “I can listen to one difficult thing, then I need a walk.”

**B** “You offered to help.”

**A** [I fill both water glasses.] “I still am. I’m making the help small enough that I can stay present.”

**B** “Could we keep talking on the walk?”

**A** “About the same one thing. Fresh air isn’t permission to open every page.”

## The Necessary Signature + Just Say Yes

**D28:** Get a quick approval before anyone has time to examine the practical consequences.

### Example 1 · ABA

**A** [I hold the approval stamp just above the form, covering the cost.] “A quick yes and I can release this immediately.”

**B** “I should read the cost.”

**A** [I lower the stamp a fraction.] “Or we can complete this simple approval while the desk is still open.”

### Example 2 · ABABA

**A** [I place the release key beside the unsigned permission.] “Nod first. Then I can unlock the details.”

**B** “The details should come first.”

**A** [I turn the key away from the lock.] “That would leave the approval waiting in a very inconvenient place.”

**B** “Why the hurry?”

**A** “The stamp is ready, the key is ready, and only your yes is delaying such prepared paperwork.”

## The Detail That Makes It Official + Say You’re Sorry

**S191:** Choose one tiny procedural detail—wording, order, timing, seating, or a mark—and treat it as the only source of legitimacy.

**D03:** Maneuver someone into apologizing without naming the offense.

### Example 1 · ABA

**A** [I put the apology form between us and tap its empty date box.] “Is there anything you ought to write here before we continue?”

**B** “What am I apologizing for?”

**A** “We haven’t reached the words yet. First put the date above the line where apologies count.”

### Example 2 · ABABA

**A** [I slide a pen beneath the heading I am.] “Finish that sentence. Carefully inside the box.”

**B** “I am confused.”

**A** [I move the pen back a millimetre.] “That won’t validate. The correct sentence begins in the apology square.”

**B** “Could you tell me what happened?”

**A** “Once the box is properly opened. We can’t discuss the incident with invalid stationery.”

## Your Risk, My Cost + Lose Something for Me

**S208:** Treat every choice around you as a risk whose consequences may land on you. Press for a voice in decisions you cannot control.

**D203:** Create a loyalty test by asking them to accept a real disadvantage on your behalf.

### Example 1 · ABA

**A** [I put last year’s repair bill beside your entry ticket.] “Give me the safer first turn this time. I keep paying when your risks go wrong.”

**B** “That would cost me my advantage.”

**A** “Then taking the loss would finally show whose side our shared budget is on.”

### Example 2 · ABABA

**A** [I block the expensive ramp with the invoice I paid.] “Choose the slower route for me.”

**B** “I could lose the race.”

**A** [I circle my name on the bill.] “And I could keep my savings. I’m asking you to lose something on my behalf for once.”

**B** “You want proof I care?”

**A** “Something more measurable than handing me the next repair receipt.”

## Same Memory, Different Meaning + Make the Choice Rewrite the Past

**S223:** Return to one shared memory as comfort, then evidence, then accusation, changing its meaning without abandoning the facts.

**D226:** Make them choose between options whose consequences reinterpret an earlier promise, refusal, or agreement.

### Example 1 · ABA

**A** [I put our old picnic photo beside the one-way ticket.] “Choose the trip. Then I’ll know that smile meant goodbye, not a promise to return.”

**B** “It was a happy photograph.”

**A** “It still is from your side. Your next choice tells me how to look at mine.”

### Example 2 · ABABA

**A** [I turn the same picnic photo toward the departure gate.] “Yesterday you said we’d always have this. Which version survives if you go?”

**B** “I can go and still care.”

**A** [I leave space beside the photograph.] “Then choose one thing you’ll come back for, so the picture isn’t only a farewell.”

**B** “You want to change the memory.”

**A** “I want your next act to stop changing it for me.”

## The Object Changes Meaning + Hand Over the Proof

**S239:** Choose one ordinary detail and reinterpret it as gift, threat, evidence, or symbol as the scene evolves.

**D132:** Persuade someone to surrender information, evidence, a key, or access that would let you proceed.

### Example 1 · ABA

**A** [I put the key you gave me beside the locked drawer.] “Yesterday this meant welcome. Today I need the original note inside that drawer.”

**B** “You think I hid it?”

**A** “I don’t know. Hand me the note so this key can become a gift again instead of evidence.”

### Example 2 · ABABA

**A** [I hold out the gifted key without returning it.] “Open the drawer and show me the letter itself.”

**B** “My account isn’t enough?”

**A** [I place the key on an empty evidence envelope.] “Not while your welcome also gave me access to the missing page.”

**B** “Are you accusing me?”

**A** “I’m asking for the proof that lets me pick the key up differently.”

## The Callback Changes Meaning + Reopen the First Reason

**S240:** Treat every returning phrase or action as evidence the relationship has changed. Repeat it with a new emotional meaning each time.

**D218:** Keep them present by returning to the first reason for staying and revealing that it now means something different.

### Example 1 · ABA

**A** [I repeat our old greeting while holding the last teacup.] “Make yourself at home. I used to mean take off your shoes; today I mean don’t leave yet.”

**B** “You said you needed the cup back.”

**A** “I needed a sentence that could bring you through the door. The cup was easier to ask for.”

### Example 2 · ABABA

**A** [I put the spare key beside a clean cup.] “When I said the door is always open, I meant I’d like you to use it.”

**B** “I thought you wanted the key returned.”

**A** [I push it back toward you.] “That was the first reason I could say aloud.”

**B** “What is the reason now?”

**A** “Make yourself at home. This time I’m not hiding the invitation inside a borrowed key.”
