# Imprompt Pack Brief — Absurd Commitment

**Pack ID:** `absurd-commitment`  
**Reserved IDs:** S145–S168 and D145–D168  
**Target release:** Imprompt v0.14.0  
**In-app status:** Playtest  
**Candidate pool:** 64 cards drafted; 48 selected; 8 held; 8 rejected

## Creative promise

**Absurd Commitment turns impossible ideas into playable reality by asking performers to believe clearly, respond specifically, and follow consequences.** The pack is not a permission slip for randomness. Its comedy comes from consistent logic: a strange premise is accepted, interpreted, protected, tested, normalized, or escalated until its practical and emotional consequences become unavoidable.

A card belongs when it helps the holder commit to the unusual while continuing to listen. The holder may become the official interpreter of an impossible event, treat a bizarre custom as shared history, calmly plan what an impossible creature eats, recruit someone into a useful delusion, or make each contradiction expand the rules of the world.

## Central comic question

> **If this impossible idea is true, how do I behave—and what must happen next?**

The card supplies a private logic or engine. It does not require the partner to share the same premise, know the same rules, or react in a predetermined way.

## What makes this pack distinct

Core Foundations introduces broad absurd and grounded lenses. Everyday Friction turns small inconvenience into pressure. Power Games asks who decides. Relationship Knots asks what remains between people. Emotional Pressure asks what feeling reorganizes behavior. Secrets & Schemes asks how information moves. Absurd Commitment focuses on **the discipline of making an unusual premise coherent and consequential**:

- impossible claims are treated as usable facts rather than disposable jokes;
- literal interpretations create concrete next steps;
- grounded players accept the premise and manage logistics;
- strange systems develop rules that remain responsive to new offers;
- escalating nonsense grows from an established pattern rather than random addition;
- emotional commitment can make belonging, fear, pride, care, and rivalry absurdly specific;
- contradictions become opportunities to clarify or enlarge the world;
- consequences matter even when the premise is impossible.

## Included territory

- literal commitment to metaphors, symbols, predictions, customs, and impossible claims;
- practical questions about maintenance, safety, timing, cleanup, cost, and responsibility;
- strange traditions, ceremonies, expertise, hierarchies, and standards;
- characters who normalize the impossible or become alarmed by the ordinary;
- bizarre systems whose internal logic can be discovered and expanded;
- competing levels of commitment to the same unusual premise;
- impossible promises, debts, tests, permissions, and negotiations;
- optimism, fear, care, pride, and belonging expressed through strange logic;
- escalating consequences that remain connected to prior offers;
- grounded reaction that treats absurdity sincerely instead of dismissing it.

## Excluded territory

This pack should not rely on:

- unrelated randomness presented as absurdity;
- ignoring, blocking, or overriding a partner’s offers;
- fixed fantasy, science-fiction, supernatural, or genre premises;
- declaring another performer’s identity, beliefs, intelligence, or private reality;
- jokes that work only once and cannot heighten;
- audience address or meta-commentary that replaces character interaction;
- a predetermined twist, transformation, creature, prophecy, or ending;
- cruelty justified solely because the world is strange;
- procedural bureaucracy better suited to Rules, Rituals & Institutions;
- complex structural callbacks better suited to Advanced Scene Engines.

## Portability rule

The prompt defines a way to commit, not the exact impossible thing.

Too narrow:

> You are the royal dragon inspector and the dragon has failed its annual exam.

Portable:

> **Impossible Specialist** — Approach the strange situation with precise technical confidence, insisting that its impossible details require exact terminology and proper handling.

The portable version can support a dragon, haunted appliance, sentient policy, impossible weather pattern, unusual family custom, or a completely mundane object treated with extraordinary seriousness.

## Hidden-information rule

A Stance may define what the holder accepts, fears, interprets literally, or treats as normal. It cannot declare that another performer is secretly magical, confused, dishonest, irrational, or committed to the same premise.

Weak:

> Your partner is an alien pretending to be human.

Strong:

> **Obviously One of Us** — Act as though effortless acceptance of the bizarre premise proves you belong, and treat visible confusion as a personal risk.

A Drive may seek commitment, explanation, approval, surrender, or proof. It cannot guarantee belief or force a shared world. Resistance should create more scene, not invalidate the card.

## Desired player experience

A player should quickly understand:

1. the unusual premise, pattern, or consequence they are attending to;
2. the first observable choice the card produces;
3. how to incorporate a partner’s unexpected offer;
4. what rule, belief, objective, or behavior can repeat;
5. how the pattern grows without becoming disconnected randomness;
6. how sincerity and specificity make the absurdity funnier.

The strongest scenes should feel like two private systems of logic discovering one shared world.

## Metadata additions

This pack adds six reusable coach roles:

`believer`, `literalist`, `normalizer`, `world-builder`, `consequence-keeper`, and `reality-tester`.

It also adds controlled motifs:

`cause-effect`, `consequences`, `impossibility`, `literalism`, `logic`, `metaphor`, `normality`, `premise`, `prophecy`, `reality`, `safety`, `scale`, `symbols`, and `transformation`.

These remain internal metadata. The visible seven-category system does not change.

## Exercise value

Absurd Commitment materially deepens existing coach tools:

- **Absurd Commitment** now draws from a much larger supply of coherent worldview and repeating-behavior cards;
- **Status Clash** gains impossible expertise, prophetic authority, strange legitimacy, and hidden practical leverage;
- **Crown & Heart** gains belonging through shared belief, protective nonsense, and emotional investment in impossible rules;
- **Past & Future** gains impossible promises, old rituals, inherited logic, and nostalgia for a more understandable reality;
- **Instigator & Anchor** gains dedicated Believer, World Builder, Consequence Keeper, Normalizer, and Reality Tester material;
- custom exercises can now target literal commitment, grounded consequences, impossible logic, and escalating world rules.

## Live-playtest questions

1. Do players build a coherent strange logic rather than adding unrelated weirdness?
2. Can grounded-response cards accept absurd offers without flattening or dismissing them?
3. Do selected cards remain playable when the partner does not share the same degree of belief?
4. Are the rules of the strange world discoverable through behavior rather than explanation?
5. Do the cards produce ordinary relationship scenes as readily as fantasy or science-fiction scenes?
6. Can beginners identify a repeatable pattern within the first thirty seconds?
7. Does Instigator & Anchor gain clearer contrast and stronger mutual support?
8. Which cards create productive literalism and which become verbal gimmicks?
9. Do consequence-focused cards keep the scene active instead of turning into planning monologues?
10. Are any prompts too similar to Core Foundations cards such as Sacred Procedure, Practical Realist, or The Grand Design?
