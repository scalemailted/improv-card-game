5. WRITE SPEAKABLE, RESPONSIVE ABABA DIALOGUE
============================================================

Five spoken turns: A–B–A–B–A.

No narrator paragraphs, gray action blocks, stage-direction prose,
or coaching explanations inside the example.

A1 establishes an immediate interaction.
B2 supplies a plausible response, fact, condition, or offer.
A3 uses B2 through the combined card behavior.
B4 changes the pressure, available options, or understanding.
A5 uses B4 to develop that same behavior.

These are functions, not fill-in-the-blank sentence templates.

Establish enough context to understand the activity, relevant
relationship, and immediate stakes. Do not force unnecessary names,
occupations, location labels, or backstory.

Small stakes are sufficient. The point is that the reader understands
why these people are responding this way.

B may cooperate, bargain, correct, reveal, concede, or resist.
B must affect A, not merely provide convenient objections.

A's response can be deliberately evasive or self-serving when
the cards motivate it. The reader must recognize that behavior,
not experience an accidental change of subject.

Characters may have irrational priorities.
The conversation must make those priorities intelligible.

Maintain stable objects, ownership, timing, responsibility, and
knowledge. Introduce material new information before relying on it.

6. APPLY ALL COACH-2.0 GATES
============================================================

Each gate receives PASS, FAIL, or UNCLEAR with specific evidence.
UNCLEAR is not writer readiness.

G1 — LEGIBLE INTERACTION
The dialogue establishes enough situation and stakes to follow it
without inventing essential backstory.

Distinguish stated facts, reasonable inference, and missing essentials.
Do not penalize harmless ambiguity or excuse essential ambiguity.

G2 — COHERENT FACTS
Material facts and referents remain intelligible. No unprepared
deadline, rule, agreement, authority, or offstage event exists merely
to rescue the final line.

Recognizable lies, mistakes, and revelations are allowed.
Accidental contradictions are not.

G3 — ALL FOUR TRANSITIONS CONNECT
Check A1→B2, B2→A3, A3→B4, and B4→A5.

Identify what each response answers, uses, changes, or purposefully
avoids. Repeating vocabulary is not enough.

G4 — PLAUSIBLE B
B has an understandable position and affects A's choices.
Would this response make sense without knowing that the writer
wants a joke in A5?

G5 — EXACT-PAIR CAUSALITY
Identify the contribution of each instruction.

The Stance changes HOW A pursues the Drive in ONE interaction.
If one card is decorative, revise.

Ask whether removing a defining instruction would remove an important
reason for A's tactic or reaction. Do not demand that no other card
could ever fit; demand that both selected cards genuinely matter.

G6 — EARNED DEVELOPMENT
A5 uses B4 to develop the established behavior.

Development may be a revealing concession, practical consequence,
changed tactic, vulnerability, or status shift. It need not become
louder, meaner, more obstructive, or more absurd.

Reject detached punchlines and convenient last-second facts.
