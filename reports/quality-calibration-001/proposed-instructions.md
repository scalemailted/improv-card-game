# Proposed additions for calibration only

Not applied to the runner, rubric, acceptance thresholds, or completed evidence. These are hypotheses motivated by the reported general complaint; exact human failures and a successful blind-reader run are still missing. Freeze these instructions before collecting held-out responses. Use the eight development cases to refine them; do not tune them from the four held-out findings.

## Writer: make the conversation legible before polishing it

1. Privately identify the shared activity, relevant roles, each person's immediate want, and what changes if they do not get it. Mark anything not established in the dialogue as an assumption. Do not make the reader need that assumption to understand a reply. Not every relationship needs a label; establish only what the exchange needs.
2. In A1, establish a playable situation and concrete request or problem naturally. Avoid spending the opening on a slogan that only makes sense after reading the card.
3. For each following turn, name the specific information or choice in the preceding line that prompts it. If a line could follow several unrelated replies equally well, examine that connection rather than adding another joke.
4. Preserve facts, referents and wants across the exchange. Introduce a change in topic, possession, authority, destination or stakes in spoken dialogue before relying on it. Distinguish an intentional character misunderstanding from an unexplained writing gap.
5. Let B pursue an intelligible immediate purpose. Cooperation, practical solutions, concessions and corrections can heighten the game. B need not manufacture obstacles.
6. Make A5 use B4's actual contribution. Heighten the same card-driven behavior through that contribution; a witty callback alone does not prove causality.
7. Keep five spoken ABABA turns initially. Allocate words to necessary context and responsive transitions before compressing for wit. No new word limit or existing gate is changed by this proposal. If a clear candidate cannot meet a current gate, record the conflict explicitly; do not silently relax it.
8. For pairs, privately complete: “A pursues [Drive] by [means altered by Stance].” The actual dialogue, including B's response, must demonstrate this merger. Do not rely on shared nouns from the two seeds.

## Reader: save comprehension before revealing the card

Stage one receives only anonymously identified dialogue. No titles, instructions, scores, prior approvals, writer explanations, human labels, seed descriptions or packet split labels.

For each example, cite actual lines for the shared activity, relevant roles, each immediate want/stakes, all four adjacent turn connections, essential missing facts, and first unclear line. “Unknown” is valid. Do not infer a charitable backstory. Distinguish ambiguity essential to understanding from harmless unspecified detail. “No unclear line found” is a model observation, not human approval.

Save and hash that output before stage two. Stage two receives exact instructions and the frozen cold read. Identify the distinctive single behavior, or how the Stance alters the means of pursuing the Drive and B responds. Compare the stages: did card text supply basic situational information missing from dialogue? Preserve disagreement; never retroactively rewrite the cold read to fit the card.

Only after these diagnostics, an eventual production reviewer would apply the unchanged coach-1.0 rubric with turn-specific evidence. A numerical pass cannot erase an unresolved comprehension finding. Calculate weighted totals in code as currently required. This calibration does not award new acceptance or invalidate old evidence automatically.

## Human comparison and held-out check

For actual matched failures, document the exact first confusing line and reported cause. Draft a small repair without changing canonical sources. Reassess the repair with a new dialogue-only reader, then card fidelity. Present originals and candidates under shuffled opaque labels, independently of their authorship or outcome. Save the permutation in a separate answer key. Collect comprehension before showing cards; never show model scores or rationales to the human reviewer.

Record agreement and disagreement with each actual human observation, not merely a preference total. Keep remaining weaknesses visible. After development decisions are frozen, apply the same instructions to held-out cases without using their outcomes to tune the proposal. Twelve examples provide a small diagnostic check, not proof of library-wide improvement. No before/after success is claimed until humans have actually supplied feedback.
